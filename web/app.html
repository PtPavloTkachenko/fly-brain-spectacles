WHAT AM I LOOKING AT · press ? or ESC to close

This is the complete brain of a fruit fly — every one of its 166,700 neurons and 25.6 million connections, mapped by scientists from a real fly — running live on your graphics card. Nothing here is an animation: each dot lights up when that neuron actually fires. The glasses (or the demo) tell it what the fly sees and smells, and the brain decides what the fly does next.

THE BRAIN
All 166,700 neurons, each lit by its own spike. Drag to turn it; arrow keys and + / − work too. The streaks are signals travelling along real connections: warm ones excite, cool ones inhibit.
NEURONS
How fast each part of the brain is firing, in spikes per second. Six regions, then the handful of “command” neurons the body listens to. Hover a row to light up just those cells in the brain.
BODY
What the brain’s decision came out as: cruise, turn, land, feed, escape. Energy is simulated; it drains in the air and refills when the fly eats.
EYES
The two compound eyes, facet by facet: amber where it just got brighter, cyan where darker. The bars are the brain’s own detectors for something looming or moving.
ROOM
Where the fly is flying: the room the glasses scanned (or the demo’s made-up one), the things named in it, and you. The inset is the room from the fly’s own head.
MEMORY
The only part of the brain that changes and lasts: 7,835 synapses in its memory centre move as the fly lives. Nobody trains it; it learns from what happens to it.
IN WORDS
On the glasses, an AI reads the numbers and writes one line about what the fly is up to.
BRAIN CHIP
The heartbeat: one step is the brain thinking through 50 ms of the fly’s life. About eight steps a second on a laptop GPU.
ADVANCED
The tuning knobs. DETAIL: how much of the brain is drawn (lower it if it stutters). PRIORITY: whether your graphics card serves the picture or the brain. THINK TIME: how long one step takes. None of them change what the brain computes.
SMALL PRINT
Region and cell names (DNa02, MN9, PPL101…) are the atlas’s own. Dimmer neurons are ones whose exact position was not imaged and is inferred. “Exposure” is a display gain and changes nothing in the spikes.
