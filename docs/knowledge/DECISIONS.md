# Decisions (ADR)

## 01 — The fly brain decides; everything else only perceives or executes
Gemini/world mesh describe *what is where*; the brain alone chooses actions via its descending/motor neurons; the duck body only executes a velocity/action command. Gemini must never emit an action ("go left") — that would make the fly decorative.

## 02 — Translate the room into fly senses, don't train from scratch
Innate reflexes are in the wiring (odour steering, sugar → feed, looming → escape, obstacle → back up). Stage 1 uses instincts only. Dopamine plasticity (KC→MBON) is an optional later experiment — no upstream repo has demonstrated learning with it.

## 03 — Chemical senses lead, vision is secondary
DOOMFLY found the biological command cells (DNa02/DNp09/MDN/MN9) silent under game-frame vision and switched to arbitrary visually-responsive cells. Our food seeking therefore rides on odour (L/R ORN drive from geometry) and taste; vision is used for obstacles/looming only if the probes show it works.

## 04 — Gemini = slow perception, geometry = fast senses
Gemini labels objects every 1–3 s → anchored 3D "sources" (food / threat / obstacle) via world mesh. Every brain tick, sensations (odour L/R intensity, taste contact, looming, obstacle) are computed from the duck's pose relative to those sources.

## 05 — Base repo: fly-wirehead, runtime outside Dropbox
fly-wirehead has the cleanest core (no trading stack; server, telemetry, checkpoints). Runtime, venv and the 1.1 GB dataset live in `$CYBERFLY_RUNTIME` (default `~/cyberfly_runtime/`, ADR 25) — Dropbox evicts/syncs large and many-file trees. Project code + docs stay in the repository.

## 06 — Transport: the plain WebSocket link — transport history since #84
Plain `ws://<mac>.local:<port>` over LAN, mDNS name published by the Python server, JSON frames with type field `t`, lens-side ping/pong + backoff reconnect. Requires Experimental APIs (lens unpublishable — acceptable). `WSClient.ts`, `ws_server.py`, `protocol.py`, `mdns.py` are reused from an earlier Spectacles project of ours.

## 08 — Maximum sensory context, for every action (Pavlo, 2026-09-11)
Don't feed the brain a minimal stimulus per behaviour. Every tick drives *all* channels we can derive — vision (duck camera view), odour L/R per source class, taste on contact, looming, obstacle/touch — simultaneously, and let the network arbitrate between actions. The probes still test channels one at a time, but only to calibrate each channel.

## 09 — Brain activity visualized on the raised hand (Pavlo, 2026-09-11)
Palm up → a point cloud of the fly's neurons appears on the hand, lit live by spikes. Positions from MaleCNS `somaLocation` (84% of retained neurons; the rest, mostly sensory, placed at the synapse-weighted mean of their located partners). Activity streams from the Mac, sparse or per-cell texture; render as a GPU-instanced point shader (see `spectacles-gpu-particles`).

## 10 — Inject each sense at the deepest level that works (from probes, 2026-09-11)
Pixels → photoreceptors does not reach behaviour in this model; the same signal injected at the fly's own feature detectors does (LPLC2/LC4 → giant fiber at 280 Hz). So the translator computes what the fly's eye + optic lobe would compute (looming, object direction) and drives those cell types directly, while still sending the camera view to R1–R8 for context (ADR 08). Taste goes straight to LB GRNs (sweet `LB3c/LB4b`, bitter `LB1*/LB2*`).

## 11 — Body = a giant 3D fruit fly, not the duck (Pavlo, 2026-09-11) — Supersedes #07
We visualize a real fly and its brain: a giant 3D Drosophila in the room that walks, turns, takes off, flies, feeds, grooms and sings — each behaviour triggered only by its brain's readouts. Brain and body now speak the same language (no fly→biped translation), and no physics twin is needed: locomotion is procedural in the lens against the world mesh. Dropped: MuJoCo duck-sim, `microduck_rl`, Rust daemons. Candidate model: DeepMind/Janelia `flybody` fruit-fly MJCF + meshes → GLB with leg/wing/head/proboscis joints. Sound from the brain: wingbeat buzz in flight, courtship song (sine ~150 Hz / pulse ~35 ms IPI) when song cells (P1 → pIP10) fire.

## 12 — Two-hand UI: right = fly status board, left = lures (Pavlo, 2026-09-11) — refines #09
- **Right hand:** cyberpunk NEON status board of all fly parameters, with two holograms: the live brain point cloud (ADR 09) and a mini fly body (body parts light up with what they're doing). Two honest sections: **NEURAL** (measured from spikes: arousal = network rate, reward PAM11, stress PPL101, fear = escape suite, hunger/approach = appetitive DNs, feeding MN9, attention side DNp66, courtship song cells, current action) and **BODY** (simulated vitals: energy drained by flight/walking, restored by eating; strength/fatigue). Never present a BODY number as a brain measurement.
- **Left hand:** lure gestures. Pinch → a sweet/fruit scent source at the hand (attractive ORNs + LC object on the hand's side) → if the brain's appetitive + steering cells respond, the fly flies over and lands on the left hand; on landing, taste contact (sweet LB) → MN9 → proboscis feeding. The fly may refuse — the brain decides. Other gestures (threat → looming, pheromone → song) to add after the atlas shows which senses drive them.

## 13 — Five flies, tabbed status board; the user is part of their world (Pavlo, 2026-09-11)
- 5 flies = 5 independent brain processes on the Mac (1 core + ~1.2 GB each). Status board gets one tab per fly; selecting a fly in the room (pinch/tap it) switches the board to its tab.
- Selected fly (its tab is open) gets: a neon glow directly under it (identification) and a MeshBuilder trail of its path; the board shows that fly's brain cloud + status.
- Lens budget for 5 flies: fly lod0 ≈ 10k tris, distance-switched to lod1 (~3k); brain cloud budgeted separately.
- The user's head (Specs camera pose) and hands are sensed entities: fast approach → looming (escape); still presence → an object the flies can orient to; hands → lures (ADR 12) or threat. Flies also sense each other as moving objects.

## 14 — Voice/Gemini commands as lures + reward, never as motor control (Pavlo, 2026-09-11)
"Fly #3, find my AirPods": Gemini (knows the room from its scans) answers via tool calling `find_object(fly, label)` → the lens marks that object as a lure for THAT fly only (attractive odour + LC object drive toward it). If the brain steers there and lands → 200 ms reward pulse into PAM11 ("benefit"). The fly does not understand words and may refuse; the brain still decides (ADR 01). With plasticity on, reward-after-arrival is also our honest learning experiment.

## 15 — Resting light pinned to the atlas calibration (from server bring-up, 2026-09-11)
The network is bistable in light (gray 153: quiet for seconds, all readouts 0 Hz; gray 160: resting DNa02_L ≈ 22 Hz within 0.3 s). Decoder thresholds and baselines are only valid at the calibration light (160/255). The weather/room "light" channel may modulate only within a narrow band around it.

## 16 — World sources: measured positions + per-class action distance (Pavlo, 2026-09-11)
Every thing a fly can sense is a **source marker** in world space: `{label, class (food|lure|threat|obstacle), position, odour radius σ, odour strength, size}`. Position comes from a depth test: Gemini bbox pixel → device-camera ray (frozen capture pose) → **World Query hit test / world-mesh raycast** (Pavlo 11.09: the world mesh IS our depth — no DepthModule), never a guessed plane (a fixed table plane misplaces anything off the table). Odour per antenna = strength·exp(−d²/2σ²) at the fly's left/right antenna (offset ±, so direction comes from the difference); visual object/loom channels from angular size and its rate of change. Radii/strengths are named tunables per class; markers + radius shells render as a debug overlay. User head/hands are live sources (threat when approaching fast, lure when pinching).

## 18 — Three flies, not five (Pavlo, 11.09) — refines #13
Brains are memory-bandwidth bound (5 processes ≈ 630 ms wall per 50 ms neural step). Three flies give each brain more bandwidth now; a batched multi-fly kernel (one shared connectome, backlog 10) is the real fix and may bring the count back up. Board tabs share the column width for any count. Re-tested on the GPU kernel (11.09): 5 flies cost lens fps, so still 3 (#23).

## 19 — Gemini objects are world sources; food near a fly is a landing target (11.09) — implements #04/#16
`WorldScanner` sends a camera frame to Gemini (RSG, `GEMINI_MODEL`) every `SCAN_EVERY_S`; boxes → world positions (device: cached depth frame, Snap Depth Cache sample; editor: World Query hit along the capture ray) → `WorldSources` food/bad/threat with floating labels, merged within 30 cm, forgotten after 150 s unconfirmed. The brains only smell/see them. Engineered and disclosed: food within `FOOD_ASSIST_CM` of a hungry fly's head becomes its landing target (altitude + touch-down) until energy > `FOOD_SATED`; the reward pulse is only for lures/commands (ADR 14) — real food rewards through taste (sweet → MN9).

## 20 — Each fly's own world + an inner voice (Pavlo, 11.09)
- **Fly eyes:** every fly casts its own ray fan against the world mesh; what reaches its brain is what its optic-lobe detectors would compute (surface time-to-contact → looming by side), never pixels (ADR 10). The world mesh is scanned in an 8 s intro (neon grid that dissolves), then only tracked.
- **Gemini runs continuously** and inventories the whole room — food, scent, bad, threat, neutral objects (phone, keys, AirPods) — as world sources.
- **Inner voice:** the selected fly's live state goes to Gemini, which speaks as the fly (a playful first-person voice, English). Shown as "GEMINI READS THE BRAIN": an interpretation layered on measured data, never a measurement, and it never feeds back into the brain.

## 21 — Faster brains = packed-state kernel, not a shared connectome (engine, 11.09) — backlog 10, refines #18
Measured (`brain_server/engine/RESULTS.md`): one process with a shared connectome and one thread per fly gives about 1.0x, so the copies weren't the bottleneck. The time went into random access to ~8 separate per-neuron arrays per synaptic delivery. `fast_advance` keeps the arithmetic and operation order unchanged and packs that state into one 32-byte cell: 2.0x at 3 flies, 2.2x at 5 (5 flies ≈ 354 ms per 50 ms step, ~0.14x real time). Spikes and full state are byte-identical to the package kernel. "Walk each spiker's edges once for all flies" was dropped: same-tick coincidence across flies is 0.3-0.7%. `parN` (one fly on N threads, exact via ordering keys) only pays with free cores (1 fly: 2.8x). Shipped behind `server.py --engine batch --kernel fast`, off by default; plasticity stays on `--engine process`.

## 22 — Flight = DN commands into our controller, not motor neurons (research 11.09) — refines #01/#11
Every published embodied fly model drives the body from descending-neuron commands into low-level controllers: NeuroMechFly v2 (2-D descending signal → CPGs; flight not implemented), flybody (pattern generator + RL torques, no connectome), Eon Systems (Shiu LIF → NeuroMechFly via DNa01/DNa02 steering, oDN1 speed, giant fibre escape, MN9 feeding — "chosen by hand"). No published work drives flight from a connectome model's motor-neuron rates. Shiu 2024: absolute rates "unlikely to be accurate", no gap junctions (the giant-fibre→TTMn/PSI synapse is largely electrical), no neuromodulation or internal state. So:
- **thrust / wingbeat amplitude ← DNg02 population** (29 cells; population code for stroke amplitude), relative to its own baseline; **turning ← DNa02 R−L** (walking role established; flight use is our extrapolation); **take-off / escape ← DNp01** itself (not TTMn/DLM);
- DLM/DVM power motor neurons at most a slow gain relative to baseline (rate → Ca²⁺ → power is the right physics, but the model's rates are uncalibrated); **steering motor neurons are NOT turn commands** (real steering is spike phase within the stroke, timed by haltere feedback the model lacks) — shown only as a cross-check;
- our own oscillator makes the wingbeat (as flybody). Honest label: "DN-decoded commands into an engineered controller".
Sources: Shiu et al. 2024 Nature (PMC11446845); Wang-Chen et al. 2024 Nat Methods (NeuroMechFly v2); Vaxenburg et al. 2025 Nature (flybody); eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation; Namiki et al. 2022 Curr Biol (DNg02); MaleCNS preprint (bioRxiv 2025.10.09.680999).
- **Follow-up (research 11.09):** nobody has natural flight from a whole-brain connectome — every demo (Eon Systems 2026 = Shiu LIF + NeuroMechFly, walking only; FlyGM; flybody) gets its natural look from a trained/engineered low-level controller + physics, the brain supplies a few DN commands. So: physics body + brain DNs.
- **Saccade DNs (all in MaleCNS, L+R):** DNa15 (excitatory, same-side) + DNb01 (inhibitory) generate spontaneous flight saccades, VES041 suppresses them (Ros, Omoto & Dickinson 2024); DNp03 gets loom (LPLC1/LC4) and triggers them (Schnell lab 2024). Plan: threshold L/R DNa15/DNb01 → a stereotyped saccade template.
- **Numbers for the body:** saccade ~50 ms, ~90° (20–180°), peak yaw ~1500°/s, 2.7–3.5/s; forward 0.26±0.08 m/s (≤0.5), climb 0.4 m/s; banked evasive turns (rotate + counter-rotate in a few wingbeats); pitch PI loop, ~6 ms delay (Muijres 2015, Bender & Dickinson 2006, Mronz & Lehmann 2008). Our flies are giants: scale speeds, keep the timing.

## 23 — GPU brain kernel = exact port with ordered deliveries, not atomics (engine, 11.09) — refines #21
Profile: one 0.1 ms tick = ~32k active-neuron updates + ~24k deliveries, and 47 % of the deliveries hit a target that gets ≥2 in the same tick, so the float summation order matters. Reversing it alone makes a stimulated fly's spikes diverge after 170 ms. So the Metal kernel uses no atomic float adds:
- active entries carry keys that reproduce the CPU's active-list order, and spikes are sorted by them;
- each target applies its deliveries sorted by the CPU's delivery number;
- `evolve()` uses the same 3 FMAs clang emits.

Result: spikes and state identical to `fast` at 1.5 s for 1 and 3 flies, except denormals, which the GPU flushes to 0 (harmless). Dense SpMV was rejected: ~1000× the edge traffic. One command buffer per 10 ms chunk covers all flies (2 dispatches per tick). ms per 50 ms step, back-to-back under load: 1 fly 41 (1.2× real time), 3 flies 73, 5 flies 123, vs `fast` 238 / 151 / 185. Shipped behind `--engine batch --kernel metal` (off by default in `server.py`; `scripts/run_server.sh` turns it on). Frozen weights only. Details: `brain_server/engine/metal/RESULTS.md`.
- **Live (11.09 evening):** 3 flies on `--kernel metal` ≈ 120 ms per 50 ms step (CPU `fast` ≈ 280 ms). 5 flies measured 190–235 ms live and the lens dropped to 13–26 fps in the editor, so the count stays at 3 (#18).

## 24 — Landing response and room walls are engineered gates, disclosed (device test 11.09) — refines #19
- **Landing on approach.** Within `FOOD_ASSIST_CM` of a landing target (food, scent/flowers, palm, lure), the eyes' loom is scaled to `LAND_LOOM_KEEP` (0.15). Otherwise the table under the apple loomed, the escape suite fired and the fly never landed: energy stayed at 0 because feeding (MN9) only happens when landed. Real flies switch looming from escape to leg extension on approach. The atlas shows this model's landing DNs don't respond to loom, so we gate it.
- **Scent sources are landable.** Flowers, plants and bins count as landing targets, not just food.
- **Room walls.** The real walls are the mesh itself (`solidWalls`: ray-test each move, keep the slide component). The scanned mesh's robust box (2–98 % per axis) is only a far backstop, padded outward by `ROOM_PAD_CM` 100, for flights out through mesh holes. It is not inset: the walls *are* the extreme vertices, so an inset box ended inside the room and pinned flies ~50×/s (11.09 preview). It also only ever GROWS and keeps being recomputed while the mesh grows: frozen at the partially scanned room its z+ face sat 50 cm from the flies and shoved them back 973 times in 25 s — the teleports Pavlo saw (12.09). It applies only once the vertex frame is verified: extracted world-mesh vertices can come back mirrored (editor: one room flipped X and Z, the next was straight), so `WorldColorBake` tests the four axis flips against real `raycastWorldMesh` hits and locks the one whose vertices lie on them (`cal=<flip>!` in telemetry). The top is at least head + 40 cm, because ceilings go unscanned. Turning away stays with the eyes and brain. It is off when the box is degenerate (editor placeholder mesh).

## 26 — Food finding: object channel = attractive things only; hunger gain on food cues (device test 11.09) — refines #19
- **Why.** Device telemetry showed `object` pinned at L=1 R=1: every Gemini source, furniture included, fed the LC12/LC10 detectors and saturated both sides, while odour was ~1e-5 at those distances. The brain had no direction to food; flies hovered 24–50 cm from it and never landed (landing needs head < `LAND_CM` 12 cm).
- **Object channel.** Only food / scent / lure drive it. Furniture is background and reaches the fly only through optic flow and eye rays.
- **Hunger gain.** `HUNGER_GAIN` 1.5: food object and odour cues × (1 + 1.5·(1 − energy)), so an empty fly gets ×2.5. An engineered stand-in for internal state (the model has none), disclosed.
- **Navigation stays with the brain.** No horizontal homing.

## 29 — Hearing: the Spectacles mic → JO-A/B `sound` via an engineered clap detector; the brain decides (11.09) — implements #08
- **Why (Pavlo: "my loud clap should stop the fly, right?").** The brain already had a `sound` channel (Johnston's organ JO-A/B, global). The atlas says JO-A/B drives DNpe007 stop +24 Hz and steer −12…−22 (a freeze), but nothing fed it.
- **Transduction on the lens, no audio to the Mac.** `FlyEars` turns loud onsets (clap, bang) into a brief pulse: the loudest 5 ms block RMS against a slow background that music and talk raise. A clap becomes a 0.5–1.0 pulse that decays over 0.3 s. The detector and its constants are engineered (disclosed); what the fly does with the stimulus is the brain's call. No body override.
- **Honest expectation.** At `sound` 1.0 (20 mV, the atlas probe) the decoder reads `stop` ≈ 0.3, below the body's 0.5 STOP gate. So a clap alone should slow or flinch a fly, and a full stop needs other stop drive (e.g. JO-C/E wind in flight). Raising the gain beyond the atlas probe would be a new disclosed call.
- **Coexists with ASK.** The mic is paused while ASR listens (#27), so the two never read it at once.

- **Correction (input audit 11.09):** in simulation, JO-A/B at 0.5 and 1.0 gave no behavioural response. The quoted "+24 Hz stop" was JO-B4_a alone. Hearing is wired but inert in this model, so don't tune `EAR_*` on the glasses expecting behaviour.
## 30 — Brain → flight after the audit: approach drive, MDN out of flight, gated habituation (11.09)
- **Thrust.** It is now resting drive + approach + a slow power-MN gain: `THRUST_REST` 0.45 + 0.65 × max(bilateral DNp66 fixation / 50 Hz, 0.8·|orient|) + power / 48 Hz + DNg02 / 20. Offline sim: an attractive object ahead raises DNp66 on both sides (+29/+32 Hz) but lowers the DLM/DVM power MNs 12–14 Hz, so the old power-only thrust fell to ~0.06 and hungry flies hovered in front of food.
- **MDN (`back`)** is driven by the object channel (LC10, +36 Hz), not by odour (±4 Hz). It is a walking command, so it no longer touches flight at all: it neither eats forward thrust nor brakes.
- **Habituation.** The baseline tau is now in simulated time (`BASE_TAU_MS` 20 s, was 60 steps = 3 s). Then **revised the same evening**: 8 s simulated (~18 s wall). Near food (odour > 0.2) only the attraction readouts freeze (DNp66 L/R, appetite, feed). Freezing every baseline turned any drift into a permanent turn: the bench's hungry flies circled the treat and never landed. Freezing on the object channel had frozen it for good, because other flies are always in view, and the slow drift read as a permanent STOP that parked every fly. The old 3 s tau had faded a target's cue within 7–13 s of wall time.
- **Hunger** now reaches a centred target: the ×0.6 is applied before the clamp.
- **Arena leash:** its "fly home" speed is no longer overwritten.
- **Solid walls** (engineered, disclosed): the part of a move that goes into the mesh is removed, a fly slides along the surface and turns its heading along it; pinning it had frozen flies (80 blocks/s).
- **Solid bodies** (engineered, disclosed, bench 12.09): two flies' thoraxes never get closer than `FLY_SEPARATION_CM` 16 (the body is 17.5 cm). A closer pair is pushed apart along the line between them; a landed fly is anchored and the flying one yields. The last escapes came from flies 3–7 cm apart, bodies overlapping, where the 1/d² loom fires even at 1 cm/s. Bench cycle after it: 6/7 PASS, `food_surface` 1 escape and energy 20 → 91, `food_air` 2 escapes and 20 → 87 (only `baseline` fails, for want of a world mesh in that editor session).

## 31 — Sense injection above the firing floor (input audit 11.09)
- **The floor.** A channel value is a voltage offset: 20 mV × v. Sensory cells fire only above about 7 mV (v ≈ 0.35) and saturate by about 0.5, and most live channels sat below that floor, so the brain never received them. Graded cues now start at `SENSE_FLOOR` 0.45.
- **Motion** (LLPC1): only the net-progressive side is driven, from the floor upward, never both sides (bilateral 0.5 pushes thrust to 1 and runs away).
- **Wind:** `WIND_GAIN` 0.25 → 0.9. At 1.0 it gives stop 0.58: the brain brakes at high airspeed.
- **Other flies:** `FLY_SEEN_GAIN` 0.6 → 0.25.
- **Touch:** bilateral, a flinch. The right side had turned the fly toward the hand.
- **Centred object** capped at 0.8 per eye; at 1.5 the stop neuron DNpe007 builds up.
- **Sweet** now goes to LB3c + claw GRNs + TPMN1/2 (proboscis contact, feed +70 Hz). LB4b is dropped: alone it drives stop.
- **Not yet done:**
  - odour ORN reselection (DC1/DC4/VL2a/VM1/VA4 by appetite; needs a combined probe);
  - moving `HUNGER_GAIN` to odour + sweet;
  - wall loom → LPLC1 with a DNp03 saccade;
  - a haltere channel.

## 32 — Landing on any surface: brain-timed, geometry-placed, off by default (11.09)
- **Why.** Real flies land on walls, ceilings and tables. In this model the landing DNs don't respond to loom (atlas: DNp07 ~0, DNp10 inhibited), so there is no brain-native "land here" signal.
- **Trigger (engineered, disclosed).** The brain wants to stop (DNpe007 > 0.4) or has lost forward drive (< 0.12) for 0.8 s, and one of 5 short eye rays (forward, down, left, right, up) hits the world mesh within 22 cm. The fly then settles on the nearest hit.
- **On the surface.** The body's up is the surface normal, so it stands vertical on a wall and upside down on the ceiling. The brain's forward drives walking (≤ 6 cm/s) and its steer turns in the surface plane. A ray along the normal keeps the feet on curved surfaces, and walking off an edge means take-off.
- **Take-off:** escape, sustained forward > 0.75 for 1.5 s, or a 40 s dwell; the fly pushes off 8 cm along the normal.
- **Status:** `SURFACE_LANDING` is false until verified in the preview and on the glasses. The telemetry shows `surf=<landings>`, and the board labels ON WALL / ON CEILING / WALKING.

## 33 — Walls loom into LPLC1 (turn away), threats into LC4/LPLC2 (escape) (11.09)
- **Why.** The eye rays' wall/ceiling loom fed the escape suite (LC4/LPLC2): 0.4 → escape 1.0 at 120 cm/s → the wall looms faster → escape again. Flies jammed in room corners, two at the same clamped point.
- **Atlas evidence.**

  | Probe | DNp03 same side | Escape cells |
  |---|---|---|
  | LPLC1 | +186 / +144 Hz | only +12 / +15 Hz |
  | LC4 | +206 / +126 Hz | +242 / +220 Hz |
  | LPLC2 | — | +104 / +97 Hz |

- **Now.** A new channel `loom_wall` carries eye-ray loom above the firing floor into LPLC1. Decode `avoid` = (DNp03_L − DNp03_R) / 120 Hz, deadzone 0.15, is added to `steer`: the fly turns away from the surface. Hands, head and fast objects keep `loom` into LC4/LPLC2, which still triggers escape.
- **Also:** buzz volume now scales with flight speed (`SOUND_SPEED_MIN` 0.5); the "BRAKE" board label is gone (MDN no longer affects flight).
- **Other flies are obstacles** (bench 12.09, `FLY_OBSTACLES`). The same time-to-contact rule as the eye rays (body radius `FLY_OBSTACLE_R_CM` 8, both flies' motion) feeds `loom_wall` → LPLC1 → DNp03, so the brain steers around a neighbour before it becomes a looming threat. Flies converging on one treat had been passing 4–12 cm apart, and the one already eating escaped. Cycles after it: `food_surface` 2 then 1 escape (was 6–13), `food_air` 0–2 (was 2–13).
- **Flies behind a wall are invisible** (12.09). The fly-to-fly object and loom channels, and the obstacle avoidance, test the line of sight against the world mesh first. A fly resting on a wall was being startled by one flying 3–6 cm away on the other side of it.
- **Only what comes AT the fly looms** (bench 11.09). The escape channel (LC4/LPLC2) gets the source's own motion toward the fly, measured from the source's previous position. A distance change caused by the fly's own flight doesn't count: that expansion belongs to the landing and avoidance system (eye rays → LPLC1). Static things (food, flowers, the lure, furniture) therefore never loom. Before this, a food run had 23 escapes from the treat being landed on, then 12–21 from flies converging on it. The atlas shows no other live input (sweet, wind, object, odour, motion) driving DNp01/02/04/11.

## 34 — Editor closed-loop bench: auto start + scenario playlist (11.09)
- **Why (Pavlo: "run lots of scenarios in the editor, auto-spawned food included, in a closed loop until the result is stable").** Hand-testing one situation at a time could not tell a fix from luck.
- **How.** `FlyScenarios` runs in the editor only (`AUTO_DEBUG`; never on the glasses). The intro ends after `AUTO_INTRO_S`, then a playlist repeats forever: baseline, food in the air (hungry), food on a surface (hungry), a looming threat, food when sated, a wall ahead, the voice-find lure. Each scenario resets the flies in front of the user (a deliberate teleport), sets their energy and seeds only the situation.
- **Verdict.** Each run ends with one `SCN_END` note in the telemetry: landings (food / lure / surface), time to food, escapes, air / landed / stuck share, speed, energy, wall clamps, mesh contacts, mean stop and forward, and PASS/FAIL against what a real fly would do, plus a running pass/run score per scenario.
- **Rule.** The bench seeds situations; behaviour stays the brain's. One change per bench cycle, so a verdict can be attributed.
- **Escapes are judged by cause** (12.09). A fly bounced off its **meal** by a neighbour is the pathology and fails the run; a startled rest on a wall or table counts with ordinary airborne avoidance, which is normal up to about one per fly per run; the threat run still has to produce escapes. Each row carries `esc=N(airX restY mealZ threatW)`. The old blanket "more than 2 escapes" rule failed good runs (4 food landings, energy 20 → 85) and passed bad ones.
- **No mesh, no verdict.** `baseline`'s "never rests" only counts when the vertex frame is verified (`cal=<flip>!`): without a world mesh there is nothing to land on, and the run would be measuring the editor session, not the fly. Such rows carry `[no mesh]`.
- **Variance.** One run is not a verdict: the same build gave `food_air` 9 escapes, then 2 (3 flies, 60 s). Judge a change by the running pass/run score over several full cycles.

## 35 — Landing response on a head-on approach (Tammero & Dickinson 2002) (11.09)
- **Why.** Flies flew 100% of the time: the brain-timed trigger (ADR 32) almost never fires, because stop stays ~0.01 in flight.
- **Rule (engineered, disclosed).** Expansion centred ahead means land, expansion off to one side means saccade away. The saccade is the brain's (LPLC1 → DNp03 `avoid`). A fly whose brain does *not* turn from a surface within `LAND_REACH_CM` ahead (|avoid| < `LAND_AVOID_MAX`, approach within ~53° of the normal) touches down on it.
- **Flight bouts.** After `FLIGHT_BOUT_S` (uniform 8–25 s, engineered for AR legibility) the fly commits to the next surface within `SURFACE_LAND_CM` of its eye rays, even if its brain is turning away. The exception: it is homing on food, a lure or a palm.
- **...and it flies to that surface** (12.09). A tired fly steers toward the nearest hit of its own eye rays (`PERCH_STEER` 0.6), takes that height and brakes on the way in. Waiting for a surface to drift within 22 cm meant a fly could cross a whole 40 s run without landing once; now `baseline` gives 5 landings and 22 % of the run spent resting, and with a real world mesh the whole cycle is 7/7.
- **Final approach.** Near a landing target, speed ≤ `LAND_DECEL_MIN_CM_S` + `LAND_DECEL_RATE` × distance: flies decelerate before touch-down, holding the time to contact (van Breugel & Dickinson 2012). Three hungry flies no longer hit the one treat at 36 cm/s.
- **Following a smell down** (12.09). A hungry fly drifts toward the HEIGHT of the nearest food it smells within `FOOD_SMELL_CM` 250 (`SMELL_ALT_MIX` 0.7 of the cruise band gives way); within `FOOD_ASSIST_CM` the ordinary landing target takes over. Altitude is engineered anyway (cruise band + landing target), and without this a treat on the real floor, a metre below the band, was unreachable: the flies smelled it but never got closer than 146 cm and starved (energy 20 → 0). After: they reach it in 5 s, land on it and feed (minFood 3 cm, 20 → 71, 65 floor contacts).
- **Hunger hysteresis.** A fly takes food as a landing target only below `FOOD_HUNGRY` 0.6, and eats until `FOOD_SATED` 0.97. With one threshold a full fly was hungry again 8 s after eating.
- **Foraging take-off** (12.09). A fly that is hungry (below `FOOD_HUNGRY`) and smells food (odour above `FORAGE_ODOR` 0.15) leaves a surface after `SURFACE_MAX_FORAGE_S` 6 s instead of sitting out the full 40 s dwell. Engineered gate, disclosed; the brain still steers the search.
- **Eased touch-down on surfaces too.** A landing from the eye rays' 22 cm reach used to move the fly that far in one frame (the jump probe caught 4 per cycle, and those snaps are what "they teleport" looked like on walls). The feet now define an anchor and the body eases onto it at `LAND_SETTLE_RATE`; walking re-casts the anchor every frame. While a fly is still easing in (farther than `LAND_SETTLED_CM` from the anchor) the edge check is skipped — its 18 cm ray would miss and read as "walked off", which churned 10 sub-second landings per 40 s. Measured after both: `jumps=0` for a whole cycle (was `surf:4`), cycle 6/7 PASS.
- **Gentle separation.** The solid-body push is capped at `SEPARATION_PUSH_MAX_CM` 4 per frame: one bench escape came from a 46 cm shove (422 cm/s), which the neighbour read as an attack.
- **Measured (12.09, 00:32).** First cycle with both: 7/7 PASS and zero escapes outside the threat run (`food_air` energy 20 → 79, `food_surface` 20 → 91, `threat` 7 escapes). The three cycles before them were 19/21.
- **Squeeze in slowly** (12.09). The landing brake also applies to the nearest other fly: within `NEIGHBOUR_SLOW_CM` 45 the speed is capped at `LAND_DECEL_MIN_CM_S` + `LAND_DECEL_RATE` × (distance − `NEIGHBOUR_CLEAR_CM` 10). At these body sizes (17.5 cm flies feeding 24 cm apart) any close pass looms hard, so a fly rushing past an eater at 31–38 cm/s from 9–14 cm startled it off its meal; flies now settle in next to each other slowly. Each fly also takes the FREE place on the ring nearest to itself, so it joins the patch from its own side instead of crossing it. Cycle after both: 7/7 PASS, no meal bounces, `food_air` 20 → 85, `food_surface` 20 → 96, `threat` 7 escapes.
- **Own spot at the food, eased touch-down.** Each fly lands on its own place on a ring `FOOD_SPOT_CM` 14 around the food's centre, one per fly. It touches down within `LAND_CM` 12 and eases onto the spot (`LAND_SETTLE_RATE` 6, ~0.4 s) instead of snapping there in one frame: a neighbour read the snap as a fly rushing at it. Measured after this change: landed flies no longer escape; the remaining escapes are mid-air near-collisions (another fly 4–9 cm away closing at ~30 cm/s), which is real avoidance. Earlier values: the ring was 7 cm, landing within 18 cm. A shared landing point stacked the flies head in head, so every newcomer loomed at the fly already eating: nearly every food landing ended in an escape, and halving `FLY_LOOM_GAIN` didn't help (6 → 9 escapes).
- **Status.** The first verdicts were inflated by the off-segment raycast bug (RUNBOOK): flies "landed" on walls metres away. Honest wall avoidance then gave 98% flight, and flight bouts are the answer to that.

## 37 — The hologram is a sample; the brain is whole (12.09)

## 39 — One clock, no per-frame garbage (12.09 device recording pass)
- **Why.** On the glasses **while recording video**: fps 8–9, worst frame 134–209 ms, `draws main=90 (mesh 23 + text 67)`. Recording costs the encoder plus thermals, so everything else has to get out of the way. Pavlo: "simplify everything you can".
- **The shape of the lens.** There is exactly ONE `UpdateEvent` (FlySwarm) and one `OnStartEvent`; every other class is a plain object ticked from inside it. Good — but it also means any 30 ms subsystem is a hard frame-rate ceiling, nothing overlaps.
- **Cut, measured from an audit of everything reachable from that tick:**
  - `FlyBody.animate` was the hottest loop: 5 bodies (3 flies + 2 easter-egg decorations) × 25 bones, rebuilding names as `"coxa_" + leg` and passing `[[axis, angle]]` literals — ~90 strings and ~200 allocations per frame. ## 40 — The board's text is one batched MSDF mesh (12.09)
- **Why.** On device `draws main=90 (mesh 23 + text 67)`: every label was its own `Component.Text`, i.e. its own mesh and draw call, re-tessellated on every `.text` write. Pavlo: "can we merge the static UI texts into a texture and one mesh?"
- **What.** A port of the author's MSDF text engine (from an earlier Spectacles lens): `SdfTextLayout` lays a string out into glyph rects with atlas UVs, `TextBatch` bakes them into ONE MeshBuilder mesh and skips the rebuild when a hash over all items is unchanged. `tools/text/build_atlas.py` bakes our OWN faces (Chakra Petch SemiBold, Share Tech Mono) into MSDF+SDF atlases — RGB = 3-channel MSDF body, A = plain SDF outline, 95 glyphs, 763×773 and 721×674, ~240 KB each — with the metadata as a TS module (LS has no runtime JSON loader).
- **Colour rides in the vertices** (uv2 = r,g and uv3 = b,a), so captions, row labels, BODY amber and Gemini violet are all one draw instead of one per colour. The board's 19 unchanging labels are queued once in the constructor and the mesh is never rebuilt.
- **Four traps, all measured:**
  - `requireAsset` resolves a `.mat` but NOT a `.png` ("Cannot find asset") — the atlas is bound **on the material** in Lens Studio and the batch inherits it, as the original engine does.
  - The generated `.mat` had an **empty `Properties` block** (dead PassInfo): the shader's uniforms did not exist, so every runtime write was a silent no-op. Fixed by regenerating the `.mat` with a fresh PassInfo UUID (same Material UUID and Pass reference) and deleting its `.meta`. **The binding then lives in LS's memory until the project is saved.**
  - Cap height must subtract `info.padding`: the glyph's atlas rect includes the generator's 8 px pad, which made every label ~20 % too small (`cap=0.865` where the true ratio is 0.70).
  - No extra UV expansion: our atlas is `-p 8` with `spacing [0,0]`, so rects already carry their padding AND touch — expanding sampled the neighbouring glyph and the labels rendered as smudges.
- **Blend additive**, not Normal: on the glasses' additive display Normal darkens the plate at every glyph edge.

## 41 — As many bones as possible driven by the brain (12.09)

- **Wings are the brain's now.** Stroke amplitude = the fly's own 67 wing motor neurons (`hz.wing`: DLM/DVM power + b1/b2/i1/i2/hg steering), auto-ranged per body against its own recent peak, with a floor of `WING_BRAIN_MIN` 0.35 so a resting fly still shows a stroke. The left/right steering MNs tilt the stroke per side (`WING_STEER_MIX`), so a turning fly beats harder on the outside wing. The 22 Hz stroke RATE stays visual and disclosed (a real fly beats ~200 Hz).

## 42 — The rest of the body on its motor pools; hunger injected; the gait stays ours (12.09) — extends #41
- **Why.** Pavlo: "hook as much as possible up to the brain — legs, grooming and so on", then "I thought the brain controlled the legs". #41 left grooming on a random timer, the antennae on a sine, the head on a command (`orient`) rather than on muscles, and never touched the leg or proboscis pools at all.
- **Measure before wiring.** Every pool was probed in this LIF model first (`brain/probe.py` harness), because DNg02 had already taught us what an anatomically-correct-but-silent channel costs:

  | pool | cells | rest | response | drives |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | DNg12 (grooming DN) | 21/side | 1.9 / 0.9 Hz | bristles +7.8 / +14.8, wind +10.5 / +15.0, sweet ~0 | the grooming bout |
  | `nm` neck | 22/side | 9 / 12.8 | sweet +9.6 / +5.9, wind −3.5 / −6.6 | head yaw |
  | `am` antennal | 6–7 | 13 / 29.7 | bristles +21, sweet −17 | antennal sweep |
  | `pm` proboscis | 33 | 3.5 | sweet +10 | proboscis extension |
  | `ad` abdominal | 107/side | 13 | wind +5 | pump depth |
  | `fl`/`ml`/`hl` legs | 58–68 each | 2–6 | ±1–3 | how hard EACH leg pushes |

- **Grooming is the brain's.** A new `bristle` sense (`^BM`, 39 L / 30 R) carries a dust load the lens accumulates from contact and wind; DNg12 decides when to clean and grooming discharges the load, so a bout starts and ends by itself. Sweet contact holds it at 0 — feeding beats cleaning, and that falls out of the connectome, not out of a rule we wrote. Ours is only which pair of legs sweeps, and that a leg cannot step and sweep at once.
- **The gait rhythm is NOT the brain's, and this is measured, not assumed.** Over 3 s of 10 ms chunks the two tripods correlate **+0.42** — they fire together — and the strongest autocorrelation is **+0.17 at 80 ms**, nowhere near the ~330 ms of a 3 Hz gait. The walking CPG is not in this model. So the six pools set stride force per leg and the tripod pattern stays ours, disclosed.
- **Hunger is injected, because the connectome has no gut.** Atlas check: every driver of the appetitive DNs is food already touching the fly (LB3c +49, TPMN1 +38, claw_tpGRN +33 Hz), and DNp09 — the walk command — rests at 0 Hz and answers nothing. A starving fly therefore had no way to learn it was empty: hunger only multiplied the odour gain, and multiplying nothing is nothing (Pavlo found a fly at ENERGY 0 % sitting still). Starvation now enters as a current into the appetitive DNs themselves, where a real fly's hunger peptides act. Deliberately circular — the `appetite` readout reads the cells we drive — exactly as `reward`/PAM11 already is.
- **The walk looked wrong for a mechanical reason.** Stride amplitude was scaled BY speed at a FIXED step rate, so a slow fly skated. Inverted: the step RATE follows speed (`GAIT_MIN`…1 × `GAIT_HZ`), the stride keeps its size (`LEG_STRIDE` 0.55 rad, `LEG_LIFT` 0.45, floor `LEG_STRIDE_MIN`).
- **Cost:** none measurable — 20 extra readouts, step still 93–96 ms.

## 44 — The Perfetto trace says the cost is our TypeScript, not the drawing (12.09)

Each eye now has its own clock, staggered a third of a period apart, and at most one reads back per frame.   - **`draws`:** 2.4 ms of PURE diagnostics — `countDraws()` walks the whole scene graph allocating two arrays per object. Behind `DEBUG_DRAW_COUNT`, off.
  - **Per-frame arrays:** `fx` and `sound` each rebuilt an argument array every frame; `FlyFx.update` ran a closure per fly with five vec3/quat allocations inside it.

If it is the readback, the rewrite is `Base64.encodeTextureAsync` (the pattern in Pavlo's own `VideoController.js`) with the Mac decoding the image and computing flow + retina.

## 45 — Rank by the MEAN, not the worst frame (12.09, closes #44)
- **The probe was ranking the wrong thing.** `perfTop()` printed `perfMax` — the worst frame in each telemetry window — so for months we optimised by spike size. Spikes were real and worth fixing, but Pavlo's "CPU usage is still very high" was about the STEADY cost, and nothing measured it. `timed()` now also sums, and `perfTop()` ranks by mean and prints `name=worst/mean`.
- **What the mean named immediately:** `bodies` **2.0–2.9 ms in every frame**, worst only 3–6 — flat, constant, the largest standing cost in the lens, and never once the top of the old spike ranking.
- **Why it cost that.** 19 bone writes per fly per frame, each allocating two or three quats — and a large share write the SAME value every frame: the six tibia at rest, the folded wings while landed, the tucked femurs in cruise, every leg of a fly standing still. Each `Bone` now remembers its last write and skips an identical one. Exact float equality on purpose: the drivers are deterministic, so a constant pose reproduces the identical number and a moving one always differs — output is bit-for-bit unchanged. Measured after: **`bodies` 1.6 ms**.

## 52 — The same exact brain on the GPU: Vulkan compute, CPU/GPU by the chunk (14.09, Pavlo: "зроби одразу GPU рішення") — extends #47 — **partly history since #87**: no GPU brain in the lens; the Vulkan kernel stays as the Mac's GPU path and the ADR 82 exactness reference
- **Why.** On a CPU a reaction is seconds; the GPU is the resource a lens leaves idle (ADR 46). The Mac server's exact Metal kernel already existed; this is its port to a portable API, and the source the page's WebGPU kernel (#55) was later ported from.
- **What.** `core/src/gpu/brain.comp` (GLSL, six entry points: begin, tickA, tickB, end, blksort, rank) + `vkbrain.cpp` (Vulkan 1.1 core only, libvulkan dlopen'ed, one descriptor set, every buffer host-visible: unified memory on Apple silicon). Exactness plan as on Metal: (epoch, seq) keys reproduce the CPU's active-list order, deliveries linked per target and applied in sequence order, clang's fma contractions written out; plus two things Vulkan needed: **64-bit keys as uvec2** (no int64 in shaders), and **division by 3 and by tau-20 as two fmas**, correctly rounded for every float (`tools/divcheck.cpp`, exhaustive over 2^32 for normal quotients), because Vulkan's own division is not required to be. No subgroup ops, no float atomics; positions from atomics never matter. Edges in 2^23-edge descriptor slices (small GPUs cap a storage binding at 128 MB); delivery records capped at 8 M per 0.1 ms tick (overflow = reported, the core drops to the CPU).
- **CPU/GPU by the chunk.** `{"gpu":f}`: f of the 10 ms chunks run on the GPU, the rest on one CPU thread reading the same interleaved edge array; the state (cells, delay queues, active list, counts, previous drive) moves between them at chunk boundaries, a few MB. So the balance is a number, and both engines are exact, so the spikes do not depend on who computed which chunk. `{"gpu_check":k}` runs k chunks on both from the same live state and compares cells / active list / queues / spike counts: a fresh machine proves its own GPU (the Mac proves it against Python).
- **Measured on the Mac (M1 Max via MoltenVK, fast math off).** Identical to Python 33/33 steps, actions to 0.000: GPU only, and half the chunks on each engine. Self-check on a warm brain: 5 chunks identical (cells equal up to the GPU's denormal flush, the Metal-known difference). Step: GPU 101 ms vs CPU serial 200 ms vs 2 threads 120 ms; a GPU chunk ~20 ms, mostly the ~200 dispatches. A side find: the CPU serial kernel reading the interleaved `Edge{post,w}` array runs 200 ms instead of 242 (one stream instead of two): worth adopting for the CPU-only build.

## 54 — The fly really sees: ommatidia -> ON/OFF -> the connectome's own optic lobe (15.09) — closes the "retina is inert" finding of #30/#31, refines #03/#10
**The retina frame was not weak, it was disconnected. Measured, not argued.**
- **Resolution.** `graph.npz` has 3,335 R1-R6 cells but only **825 distinct viewing directions**, and at the 8x16 frame `FlyVision` sent they collapse into **81 distinct pixels**. The complete layer is the LAMINA: L1/L2/L5 each have one cell per column with a MaleCNS hex assignment — **875 columns on the left eye, 892 on the right**, shared between the types 1:1. R1-R6 covers only 299/875 and 525/892 of them.
- **Saturation.** The model's photoreceptor drive is `30*lum/(0.02+lum)`. At ADR 15's calibration light (grey 160 = linear 0.3515) that is **28.385 mV**, and the ENTIRE bright half of the display range moves it by **+1.03 mV** (grey 200 = +0.61, grey 255 = +1.03) against the ~7 mV firing floor of ADR 31. The semisaturation constant sits 17x below the resting luminance, so the eye is pinned in saturation before anything is drawn.
- **Sign.** R1-R6 is **purely inhibitory in this model** (mean weight -4.63, 0 % positive edges), and so is L1 (-5.71, 0 %). Nothing a picture does can EXCITE the medulla.
- **The measurement that settles it.** Under EVERY image we could draw into that frame — static and moving, dark and bright disc, grating, noise, a looming disc — **Mi1, Tm2, Tm9, every T4, every T5, LC4, LPLC1, LPLC2, LC11, LC12, LLPC1 all sat at 0.00 Hz**, exactly as at rest. The one thing the frame did was non-specific: any textured image pushed R1-R6 from 20 to ~126 Hz within seconds (ADR 15's bistability) and saturated the body readouts identically for every stimulus. **The picture had no information path at all.**
- **A fix at the photoreceptor is NOT enough (measured).** Giving R1-R6 the full per-column lattice AND moving its operating point onto the semisaturation constant with a Weber adaptation left the optic lobe at 0.00 Hz in every scenario. The break is at lamina -> medulla.

**What the connectome does have**, from its own edge sums: `L1 -| Mi1` (-39,101, Mi1's strongest input) with `L5 -> Mi1` (+21,080) and `L3 -> Mi1` (+9,804) = the ON pathway; `L2 -> Tm1` (+56,493) and `L2 -> Tm2` (+60,826) and `L3 -> Tm9` (+13,519) = the OFF pathway; then T4/T5 -> LPLC2, T2/TmY3/Tm4/Tm2 -> LC4, and T3/T2/Tm6/Tm12 -> LC11 with **no T4/T5 input at all** — Borst & Helmstaedter's ON/OFF split, Klapoetke 2017's LPLC2, Ache 2019's LC4 and Keles & Frye 2017's non-motion LC11, wired and intact. Only the drive was missing.

**So: one camera per eye, the model's own ommatidia, and inject at the lamina.**
- **Lens (`FlyRetina.ts`).** Two cameras per fly on FlyVision's private vision layer, aimed +-`RETINA_EYE_YAW_DEG` 35 deg (~30 deg binocular overlap, as in Drosophila), 100 deg FOV, 32x32 (~4.3 deg per texel at the centre = one ommatidium). Each of the 1,767 columns is projected through its camera ONCE at build time into a fixed texel, so a sample is a flat loop. Per column: Rec.709 luminance -> `log` -> minus its own running mean (`RETINA_TAU_S` 0.15 s) = Weber contrast, clipped at `RETINA_FULL` and quantised to one signed byte. One GPU->CPU readback per frame at most, staggered across every eye of every fly, at `RETINA_HZ` 6.
- **Lattice (`brain_server/eye.py`, ONE source for all three sides).** MaleCNS hex -> `x = h1 - h2/2, y = sqrt(3)/2*h2` times `DPHI_DEG` 5.0 (Drosophila 4.5-5.5, Land 1997). The lattice then spans **142 x 165 deg per eye** — the real eye, to the degree. `export.py` writes the same arrays into the `.flyb`, and `python eye.py --ts` writes the lens's `FlyRetinaData.ts`, so the lens, the Python worker and the C++ core cannot drift.
- **Wire.** `senses.eye` = base64 of 1,767 bytes, 128 = no change. A column at exactly 128 is skipped by BOTH implementations (an integer test), so a still scene costs the kernel nothing.
- **Injection.** ON = the positive half, OFF = the negative half. `L1, L2 += mv*OFF` (the sign-inverting LMCs), `L5 += mv*ON`, `Mi1 += mv*ON` (the ON pathway's first medulla neuron), `Tm1, Tm2, Tm9 += mv*OFF`. **Everything after those seven cell types is the connectome computing for itself** — direction selectivity, looming, small objects, the descending neurons.
- **The gain is ours and disclosed:** `MV_LAMINA = MV_MEDULLA = 120` mV per unit of normalised contrast (cf. `MAX_MV` 20 for the engineered channels, the model's own `lamina_bias` 12 and its 30 mV photoreceptor drive). Why it takes that much: one L2 spike puts **4.3 mV** into Tm1 (weight 27.25 = 99 contacts x 0.275 mV, peak EPSP factor `(a-b)/3` = 0.157) against a 7 mV gap to threshold, so every stage of the chain needs two coincident spikes inside ~10 ms. Shiu et al. 2024 say this LIF model's absolute rates "are unlikely to be accurate"; the gain buys back the photoreceptor gain the model never had.

**What the eyes can SEE (15.09, Pavlo: "the eyes must also see the user's hands and the other flies, so the fly reacts accordingly").** The eye cameras render one private layer, so until now that was the room and nothing else. Now:
- **The room** was already there and stays: `hideWorldMesh` puts the tracked world mesh on the vision layer wearing `WorldColorBake`'s baked room colours. **This is why no Device Camera Texture background is used, and it is not a shortcut.** That texture is a 2-D image taken from the USER's head; pasted behind a fly's eye it would have the wrong parallax, the wrong occlusion and would inject optic flow the fly is not producing — a lie the LPLC2/T4/T5 detectors would answer to. The baked mesh is the real room, geometrically correct from the fly's own viewpoint, and it costs no second full-scene render because the eye cameras already draw it.
- **The other flies.** Each fly body gets its own `LayerSet.makeUnique()` **added** to the layers it already had (so the user still sees it), and eye camera *i* renders every body layer EXCEPT its own — a fly never sees the inside of its own thorax. Multiplayer ghosts (ADR 53) go on one shared layer that every eye renders, since a ghost is never anyone's own body; `FlySwarm` calls `retina.addGhost(container)` where the ghost is built.
- **The hands.** Sphere proxies on the tracked joints (palm, index tip, thumb tip, per hand) on a layer **only the eyes render**, so nothing appears in the main view. SIK's own hand visuals were rejected: they do not exist in the editor at all and re-layering another package's prefab is fragile. A blob is enough — what LPLC2/LC4 answer to is an EXPANDING EDGE, not a shape.
- **Verified in the preview, measured** (`RETINA_HAND_DEBUG`, the seeder below). ## 62 — Teaching the fly = the paper's own conditioning session, run in the room (15.09, Pavlo: "відтворити оригінальні алгоритми навчання") — extends #02, #14, #48, #59

**The rule was always there; the PROTOCOL never was.** `rule.py` (fly-wirehead) and `plasticity()` (flybrain.cpp) are, line for line, appendix equations 3.2-3.5 of **Huang, Luo et al. 2024, "Dopamine-mediated interactions between short- and long-term memory dynamics", Nature 634:1141 (doi 10.1038/s41586-024-07819-w)**. What no repo in our lineage ever shipped is the trial structure that turns a rule into learning. We read all three upstreams and the paper, and this is what is actually in them:

| Where | What it contains |
|---|---|
| `flywirehead/neural/rule.py` | the centred anti-Hebbian rule, traces `trace_kc/dan 1.0 s`, memory `1800 s`, weight filter `0.05 s`, efficacy bounds `0.1-2.0x`, bin <= 10 ms. Inherited verbatim from stonkfly (hash in `upstream.json`), which took it from DOOMFLY |
| `flywirehead/neural/circuit.py` | the two compartments: **PAM11 (15) -> MBON07 (4)** and **PPL101 (2) -> MBON11 (2)**, all 7,835 existing KC->MBON edges, DAN->MBON gain = anatomical contact fractions. Its own docstring: "Using the same centered rule in both is a new, unvalidated model assumption" |
| `flywirehead/engine.py` | the **only** US in the lineage: `stimulate()` = a 200 ms, 20 mV pulse into PAM11; plus the continuous `video_reward` drive |
| `flywirehead/cli.py` | the two controls: `--frozen` (weights fixed, traces still run) and `--no-video-reward` |
| `tests/test_full_connectome.py` | the nearest thing to a protocol: "the upstream assay's 1.5 s conditioning period" (3 x 500 ms CS), then a 100 ms CS test against a different stimulus, then control / paired / frozen. A mechanism assay, not a behaviour protocol |
| **nowhere** | trials, inter-trial intervals, a CS- control, a before/after measurement, an appetitive protocol |

**The paper's session, which is what we reproduce** (Methods; the paper is head-fixed on a trackball with voltage imaging, aversive only):
- **CS** 30 s of odour in training, 5 s in the test. Two attractive odours per fly, counterbalanced: one is CS+, the other CS-.
- **US** electric shock starting at a **fixed CS+ -> US interval of 3 s**, 16 pulses of 0.2 s at a 2 s period (~28.8 s), so the US sits INSIDE the CS.
- **6 training bouts**, CS+ then CS-, 135 s of air between presentations (the model's optimum is 360 s; real flies confirmed 360 beats 60 and 900).
- **Measurement** not a T-maze and not a performance index, deliberately: "we measured behavioural responses to the CS+ and CS- before, during and after conditioning, instead of assessing the conditioned response at only one time point". The readout is **rotational velocity toward the odour**, and the bias is defined as the CS+ minus CS- response **minus that same fly's before-training value, which is zero by definition**.

**The AR mapping** (`FlyTrainer.ts`, `TRAIN_*` in FlyConfig, session UI in `FlyGuide`):
- **CS = a thing in the room.** ASK names it (ADR 27); the trainer then owns its own lure on that thing, which feeds `odor{L,R}` + `object{L,R}` for that fly only. A second known thing becomes the **unpaired CS-** automatically (`TRAIN_AUTO_CS_MINUS`) — without it there is no discrimination, only a session.
- **US appetitive** = `pulse "reward"` -> 20 mV into the 15 PAM11 cells. **US aversive** = `pulse "punish"` -> the 2 PPL101 cells, plus a real `threat` source rushed at the fly so its own LC4/LPLC2 loom fires and the user can SEE the US. Both are fired by the trainer, never by the landing hook.
- **The US is inert without plasticity, by the model's own construction.** PAM11 and PPL101 are in `modulation_mask`, so the kernel delivers no synaptic current from them (`flybrain.cpp:979`) and the `--compact` brain file drops their edges entirely. A reward pulse therefore changes NOTHING except through the rule. The TEACH key turns learning on first and the trainer waits for `memory.learning` before a bout may count.
- **Session** probe CS+ / probe CS- (nothing follows: the before-training zero) -> 6 bouts of paired CS+ then unpaired CS- -> the same two probes again.
- **Measurement** the paper's, as closely as the wire allows: `act.steer` (DNa02 + DNp66 + DNp03) signed toward the CS side, sampled only over the **first 5 s of the BRAIN's own clock** after CS onset — `msg.sim_ms`, `TRAIN_BIAS_SIM_MS`, the paper's 5 s test bout — because after ~8 s the decoder's baselines creep (`BASE_TAU_MS`). Plus the two things AR adds and a trackball cannot: **did it go**, and **how long it took**. Plus the brain's own `memory{mean_efficacy, changed}` (baseline exactly 1.0, 7,835 edges).

**Exact, and approximate, stated separately.**
- **Exact:** the rule and its constants (verified C++ == Python, ADR 47/48); the compartments and the real KC->MBON edges; the US amplitude (20 mV) and the 200 ms pulse, which are fly-wirehead's own; the 16-pulse US train; the 6 bouts; the fixed 3 s CS -> US interval, counted on `sim_ms`; the CS+/CS- pair; the before/after probe design and the bias definition; the two controls (learning off = `--frozen`, extinction = `--no-video-reward`).
- **Approximate, and why:** the **US period** is compressed 2.0 s -> 0.5 s, because at 1-2 s of wall time per 50 ms brain step a faithful train would be minutes of the user standing still (and in wall time the train collapses into continuous drive anyway, which is exactly upstream's `video_reward` mode). The **3 s CS -> US interval is capped** at `TRAIN_US_DELAY_MAX_S` wall seconds for the same reason. The **inter-trial interval** is 10 s, not 135; the paper's 360 s optimum comes from its STM->LTM gating (`w_MD,1,3 = -0.382`, MBON-γ1pedc -> PPL1-α3), and **this rule has no such gating** — one KC->MBON layer, a fixed DAN gain matrix, no MBON->DAN feedback — so there is no ISI optimum to miss. The **test is 20 s of room, not 5 s of odour port**, though the bias inside it is read over the paper's 5 s of brain time.
- **What the model cannot do, said plainly.** The paper's appetitive case does not exist — it models **PPL1 only** (γ1pedc / α'2α2 / α3) and names appetitive learning as future work. So our **aversive protocol is the faithful one** (PPL101 -> MBON11 IS the paper's STM compartment γ1pedc), and the **appetitive one is fly-wirehead's own extension** of the same rule to PAM11 -> MBON07, disclosed as such by `circuit.py` itself. The paper has **no efficacy bounds** (its ceiling is on MBON rate, `M_MBON`); the 0.1-2.0x clamp is fly-wirehead's declared choice. And the deepest limit: **in this reconstruction the KCs are not odour-sorted**, so two room things do not land on different Kenyon cells. Any efficacy change is real, but it is not guaranteed to be CS-specific. The trainer therefore logs `kc=<Hz>` (`regions.mushroom`) on every presentation, so the question "did the cue reach the mushroom body at all" is answered with a number, not an opinion.

**Measured (preview, 15.09, `debugScenario auto_solo+train`, editor session of 2 bouts, one full run end to end).**

## 63 — The memory survives the session: the plastic state is a blob the lens owns (15.09, Pavlo: "ми маємо бекенд щоб зберігати зміни в мозку") — extends #48, #62, #55
- **Why.** ADR 48 gave the brain a real learning rule and ADR 62 gave it a real training session, but the result died with the lens: a fresh `fb_create` starts every KC->MBON synapse at its baseline. Teaching only means something if the fly is still the fly you taught.
- **What travels.** The rule (fly-wirehead `rule.py`, `plasticity()` in `flybrain.cpp`) has exactly four state arrays: `mem_u` per plastic edge (the slow trace, tau 1800 s — this IS the memory), `mem_w` (the fast follower the weight is written from, tau 0.05 s), `rate_kc` per edge and `rate_dan` per DAN cell (1 s rate traces). **The weights themselves are never stored**: `weight = pl_base * (1 + mem_w)`, recomputed on load, so a memory can never contradict the brain file. All four are float64 because the rule runs in float64 — float16 or 8-bit would not round-trip, and the check below would fail by construction.
- **Core commands** (`core/src/flybrain.cpp`, the same core in `server.py --engine native` and in WASM):
  - `{"memory":"get"[, "compact":true]}` -> a queued `{"t":"memory","op":"get","data":"<base64>",...}`.
  - `{"memory":"set","data":..,"elapsed_s":X}` -> load it, aged by X seconds off.
  - `{"memory":"clear"}` -> naive again, without touching the neural state (`{"reset":true}` still wipes both).
  - `{"memory":"echo"}` -> save and load back **in the same instant**: the memory's own self-check, the twin of `gpu_check`.
  - The reply message also carries `rewards` / `punishes` (US pulses delivered) and `since_ms` (brain time since a weight last moved); the `brain` message's `memory{}` block gained the same three.
- **A side channel, so nothing is dropped.** `fb_take` used to hold one message and drop an unread one. A blob must not be lost that way, so the core now has a small never-dropped queue read first by the same `fb_take` — **no new module export, so the device path needs only a rebuild, not an API change.**
- **Format** `FMEM` v1, deflated (`FMEZ`) then base64. 56-byte header (magic, version, flags, edge and DAN counts, a fingerprint of the plastic edge set, the count of stored edges, the brain clock, the age of the last change, the two US counters) then u16 indices and the float64 arrays. **Sparse:** a KC that never fired leaves `drive` exactly 0, so its numbers stay exactly 0 and are not written — lossless and about half the size. The fingerprint makes a blob from a different brain file (`brain_c0` vs `brain_hot` renumber the neurons) fail loudly instead of poisoning the synapses.
- **`compact` = what a gap destroys anyway, dropped on purpose.** It leaves out the two 1 s traces (multiplied by `exp(-gap / 1 s)` on load: below 1e-17 after 40 s) **and `mem_w`** — because `mem_w`'s own contribution to its future is `w0 * exp(-gap / 0.05 s)`, below 1e-16 after two seconds, and everything else about `mem_w` is a function of `mem_u`, which the load computes. So the cross-session blob is **`mem_u` alone: 10 bytes per moved synapse instead of 26**, and it is exact, not approximate. Measured: compact and full give the identical mean efficacy after the same 60 s gap (0.962653000 both). The reply carries `approx:true` in the one case where it would not be — a compact blob loaded with less than 2 s of gap.
- **The memory keeps fading while the glasses are off, and that is the honest answer.** The rule's own tau was 1800 s (10800 s = 3 h since 21.09, ADR 92). A stored memory therefore carries a timestamp and a load carries `elapsed_s`; the core applies the decay in closed form — and with no KC and no DAN spikes the discrete recursion has an exact closed form (`u(t) = u0 e^{-t/tu}`, `w(t) = w0 e^{-t/tw} + u0 * tu/(tu-tw) * (e^{-t/tu} - e^{-t/tw})`), so this is **not an approximation of the rule, it is the rule advanced over the gap**. Measured on the trained blob: 300 s off leaves 85 %, 1800 s leaves 37.1 % (= 1/e, as it must), 1 h leaves 14 %, 7 h leaves nothing. Teaching is worth repeating, exactly as it is for a real fly. The alternative — freezing the memory while the lens is closed — would have been the engineered override, not this.
- **Where it lives (`FlyMemory.ts`).** Primary and canonical: the device's own persistent storage (`global.persistentStorageSystem.store`), the blob as **raw bytes** (`putUint8Array`, a third smaller than its base64). **That store is 102,400 bytes in total** (`getMaxSizeInBytes()`, measured on this device 15.09) — which is exactly why the compact form had to exist: at 26 bytes per synapse a trained fly would not have fitted, at 10 it is ~35 KB for a normal session and ~74 KB even if all 7,835 moved. The lens holds the copy and hands it to **whichever engine is the brain** — the in-lens core, a web page (ADR 55) or the Mac socket all take the same `memory` message — re-pushing whenever `msg.engine` changes. While a page is the brain the lens pulls a fresh copy every `MEMORY_PULL_S`, because a page can vanish without warning; that is the most learning a crash can cost. Saves fire on `MEMORY_SAVE_CHANGES` new synapses (never oftener than `MEMORY_SAVE_MIN_S`), when learning is switched off, on `FlyTrainer`'s session end, and on `OnDestroyEvent`.
- **The key is the USER, not the device (Pavlo, 15.09: "щоб по айді юзера підключалось до тієї ж мухи і того ж мозку").** Sync Kit's `UserInfo.userId` is documented as "unique per lens for each user", so the same person on another pair of glasses is the same fly; it only exists once a session is ready, so `FlyMemory` polls for it and falls back to a random per-device uuid, and `status().keySource` says which is in use. **Finding:** the editor's mocked single-player session answers with a placeholder beginning with `mock`, which would be one shared id for everybody — it is rejected, so the preview always runs on the device key and the user-id path can only be confirmed **on the glasses**. Only four characters of an id are ever logged.
- **"The same fly", not just the same weights.** The record also carries an identity — name index, colour index, birth date, finished sessions, and how often each labelled thing was rewarded or punished — drawn once on the first run and never again. So a user who puts on other glasses meets THEIR fly, with its name and its history, not a stranger with familiar synapses. `FlyMemory.flyName() / nameIndex() / colorIndex()` are what the namer and colourer read.
- **Optional backend mirror,** off by default and never needed: one Supabase table `fly_memory(fly_id text primary key, blob text, identity jsonb, saved_at)` written through PostgREST with the anon key. No accounts and nothing about a person: the key is that per-lens user id. `MEMORY_BACKEND*` in `FlyConfig`, table, RLS and the honest note on what anon-key policies can and cannot protect in `web/DEPLOY.md`. Keys are never committed.
- **`reset()`** (the trainer's RESET key) is one call: core `{"memory":"clear"}`, the store wiped, the backend row deleted, one `MEMORY_RESET` line, and `justReset` true for two seconds so the board can say FORGOTTEN. The fly keeps its name and colour — same fly, knows nothing again.
- **Proof** (`core/tools/memtest.py`, Mac core, `brain_c0`, 2 threads, 15.09):
  - *Lossless and non-perturbing:* the same 60-step conditioning session run twice, once with `memory:echo` in the middle -> **identical spike totals 60/60, worst mean-efficacy difference 0.000000000**.
  - *The memory is real:* one blob, then the same probe twice from the same reset state with learning off — naive vs loaded. Not a rounding difference: `act.feed` +0.251 -> +0.724, `thrust` 0.515 -> 0.242, `feed` 10.2 -> 14.9 Hz, `DNa02_L` 14.6 -> 9.7 Hz, 923 fewer spikes. The trained fly eats where the naive one flies on. (This also answers ADR 62's "the memory's output is invisible": MBON07/11 are not readouts, but what they drive is — the decoder sees it.)
  - *Refusal:* a foreign or short blob is rejected (`ok:false`), and `FlyMemory` then drops the stored copy rather than train on nonsense.
  - *Sizes and cost:* 3,699 of 7,835 synapses moved after 6 rewards -> full form 76 KB deflated / 102 KB base64; **compact 34.9 KB / 46.5 KB base64** (9.4 bytes per moved synapse). A `get` costs the brain thread one step's worth of pause (~30 ms of work on an M1 inside a 120 ms step).
  - *WASM + WebGPU:* the same four commands in `web/test.html` under headless Chrome (`apple metal-3`, 91 ms/step): `echo` 71,691 B ok, `get compact` 33,057 B, `set` with `elapsed_s` 1800 moved the efficacy 0.987746 -> 0.995536 (36 % left, as the tau says), `clear` back to 1.0.
  - *End to end in the running lens, through the relay:* the live preview with a real web page as the brain produced `WEB_BRAIN_ON page=web macOS` -> `MEMORY_SAVE bytes=... ` -> `MEMORY_INIT ... stored=...B age=173s` -> `MEMORY_LOAD engine=native agedS=173` across preview restarts. The lens, the page and the device store all speak the same blob.
- **A bug found on the way.** The core's inbox was only drained inside `step()`, so once `{"pause":true}` (ADR 55) stopped the stepping thread, `{"pause":false}` could never be read: the core could be paused but never woken. It never bit in practice, and it would have on the next rebuild. The paused loop now drains too.

## 64 — No second learning rule in the central complex: the cells are there, the identities are not (15.09) — closes the PFL3 question of #63
- **Asked.** Add the goal-heading rule of the Dan / Hulse / Jayaraman / Hermundstad line of work — dopamine-gated plasticity onto PFL3 from the compass — as a second rule beside KC->MBON, off by default.

## 66 — Seven sessions, one plan each: every protocol the rule can support, picked from the guide card (15.09, Pavlo: "продумай ВСІ сценарії навчання") — extends #62, #65

**Why a set and not a mode flag.** ADR 62 built one protocol. The rule supports more, and a rule with
only one protocol cannot be checked: you cannot tell learning from drift without an unrewarded test,
you cannot tell a memory from a preference without a choice, and you cannot repeat an experiment
without a way to wipe it. So the trainer now carries the whole set, and the three that only ASK a
question **freeze the synapses while they ask** — a measurement may not teach.

**One plan per mode.** A session is a flat list of presentations built when it starts (`Step[]`), so
`tick()` has no session logic and a new protocol is a new plan, not a new state machine.

| mode | CS+ | CS- | US | bouts | the test measures | success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **food** (appetitive) | the named thing | the next known thing, shown as often, never paid | 16 x `pulse reward` -> PAM11, 3 s after landing | 6 | steering toward each cue, before and after | cue's bias up MORE than the control's; `DIFFERENCE +x` on the card |
| **danger** (aversive) | " | " | 16 x `pulse punish` -> PPL101 + a real loom | 6 | " | cue's bias DOWN relative to the control |
| **forget** (extinction) | " | — | none, ever; **weights stay free** | 6 | bias and `mean_efficacy` across the session | bias falls back, efficacy climbs toward 1.0 |
| **test** | " | " | none; **learning OFF for the session**, restored after | 3 repeats | mean CS+ bias vs mean CS- bias | cue > control, and repeatable, because nothing moved |
| **choice** | " | " | none; learning OFF | 6 | which cue it reaches first, both lured at once | cue picked > 50 % of the trials it answered |
| **transfer** | " | " | none; learning OFF | 3 repeats | the same, plus what `FlyMemory` restored at start | the card names the synapses and their age, then the bias |
| **reset** | — | — | — | — | — | `FlyMemory.clear()`; the card says how many synapses had moved |

**The three that needed a real decision.**
- **forget keeps plasticity ON.** Extinction is not the off switch. In this rule, unlearning is the
  same equations running with the DAN silent — turning learning off would freeze the memory, which is
  the opposite of forgetting. (`--frozen` remains available as the board's plain toggle.)
- **test and choice turn it OFF and hand it back.** Otherwise the after-measurement of a FOOD session
  is itself another presentation of the cue, and every test slowly becomes extinction. This is the
  single most important honesty fix in the set.
- **choice needs two cues at once, and the lens has ONE lure slot.** Both cues are real lure sources
  of equal size and strength, but only one can hold FlySwarm's slot (and therefore the landing gate),
  so **the slot swaps every trial** — the same cancellation a side swap gives. The measure is
  therefore first APPROACH within `TRAIN_NEAR_CM`, not a landing, and each trial logs its own starting
  distance so an unfair geometry is visible. A fly that reaches neither is a refusal, not a vote.

**The picker.** Seven keys on the guide card, two columns. Every quad is created in the FlyGuide
constructor — a NeonBatch sizes its mesh at the first `flush()` and a quad added later has no
vertices (RUNBOOK) — and the picker only toggles their visibility flags and their hit boxes. The
labels ride in the card's existing batched-text mesh, so the picker costs **no new draw call and no
new Text**; the live line stays the one per-frame string. `TEACH THE FLY` opens the picker;
`FlyBoard.onTrainMode` forwards the key to `FlyTrainer.choose`.

**Each mode owns plasticity, not the key.** `onLearn(true)` no longer forces learning on: it opens
the picker, and the chosen mode decides. Without a trainer the key is still ADR 48's plain toggle.

**The page sees it too.** `TrainState` (mode, phase, n/N, cue, control, bias before/after, efficacy,
the live line, the result) goes into `FlySceneFeed` as a `train` field, sent **only when it changed**,
exactly like the `th` thought line. FlySceneFeed belongs to the web agent: a field and a diff block,
no signature change.

**Bench.** `debugScenario` accepts `train_danger | train_forget | train_test | train_choice |
train_transfer | train_reset` (plain `train` = FOOD): the bench presses the picker key at 20 s and
names the cue at 23 s, which the user's finger and ASK do on the glasses.

**Verified in the preview (15.09).** `TRAIN_MENU` -> `TRAIN_START fly=0 mode=danger (aversive
conditioning) bouts=2` -> `TRAIN_CS cs+='sugar cube' (odor_dm2) cs-='blue mug' (odor_va2, moved
apart)` -> probe rows with `learn=on`. The picker renders (screenshot: its right column, CHOICE /
TRANSFER / RESET). **The guide card still cannot be read whole in the editor preview** — it sits left

## 70 — The ocelli reach the ocellar ganglion, not the neck: the righting reflex stays engineered (15.09) — extends #54, holds #22
**What was asked.** Replace `FlyBody`'s engineered roll/pitch (`BANK` 0.35, `PITCH_FWD` 0.22, `PITCH_HOVER` 0.08) with a brain-driven righting reflex, fed by a new ocelli sense. **Measured first, and the gate failed — so nothing in `FlyBody` was touched.**

**What was built and IS good (shipped in the export).** A sided `ocelli` sense channel on OCG01a/b, OCG02b/c, OCG03 (6 cells a side). MaleCNS has the ocellar ganglion but **not** the ocellar photoreceptors (OCG01a's only inputs are other OCG cells and central neurons), so this is the same disclosed pattern as ADR 54: we supply what the missing receptor layer would supply. Biology cited for the PATHWAY, not for any number: the ocelli are dorsal brightness-gradient detectors driving a fast gaze/body righting reflex through the neck motor neurons (Taylor 1981, locust; Parsons, Krapp & Laughlin 2010, ocellar L-neuron -> descending -> neck, blowfly).

**The drive knee, and a pleasant surprise: no new gain constant.** Sweeping the channel (ipsilateral v, contralateral 0.25v), OCG01a_L / OCG01b_L / OCG02b_L / OCG02c_L / OCG03_L in Hz:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| OCG03 | 0 | 30.7 | 61.3 | **87.3** | 126.7 |

Threshold sits between 0.25 and 0.50 - i.e. between 5 and 10 mV, exactly ADR 31's ~7 mV sensory firing floor - then it is near-linear. Laterality is clean to v = 1.0 and **breaks at 1.5**, so **v = 1.0 = the standard `MAX_MV` 20 mV is the knee**: unlike the compound eye (which needed 120 mV, ADR 54) the ocelli need no special gain at all. Roll flips sign with the side and grows monotonically; pitch is the common mode, monotone and saturating past v = 1.0, and lateral-symmetric. Offline, both axes work.

**Built:** an upward 8x8 camera per fly folded into the same one-readback-per-frame scheduler (the compound eye cannot do this job - its cameras are 100 deg at 0 deg pitch, so their top row is only ~+50 deg elevation, dorsolateral, which would confound roll with yaw). One SLOW shared adaptation (`OCELLI_TAU_S` 4 s) on purpose: a roll moves the halves apart, a pitch moves both together, room brightness divides out - a fast adaptation would erase the pitch signal with it. Live in the preview: `oc=0.54/0.62`, sitting on `OCELLI_BASE` 0.55 inside the measured usable range.

**THE LIVE LOOP FAILS, and the reason is the readout's resolution, not the sign.** Forcing a known attitude and reading the telemetry:
| forced bank | ocelli L/R | `rightRoll` |
|---|---|---|
| **+0.45 rad** | 0.54/0.47, 0.55/0.50, 0.55/0.51, 0.57/0.51 (L brighter, consistent) | **-0.309, -0.162, -0.130, -0.036** (consistently negative) |
| **-0.45 rad** | 0.55/0.53, 0.56/0.53, 0.56/0.54, 0.57/0.52 (**L STILL brighter**) | **-0.289, +0.024, -0.310, +0.498** (no consistent sign) |
The +0.45 case is textbook and confirms the sign: left brighter -> `rightRoll` negative -> `bank = RIGHTING_GAIN * rightRoll` opposes the disturbance, negative feedback, positive gain. **The mirror does not flip.** Two causes, both measured: (1) this room's dorsal field is statically asymmetric by ~0.05 in `oc`, larger than the +-0.04 the roll itself modulates; (2) **DNp20 is ONE cell per side** - at 150 ms smoothing its rate is quantised in ~7 Hz steps (the raw pairs were 7/20, 12/24, 9/18, 20/36, 15/15), so the roll signal's noise is the size of its own range. Pitch was worse (-0.44, -0.17, -0.40, +0.33): the common mode tracks the room, not the attitude.

**Way 1 (Pavlo 15.09): pool more descending cells - and the measurement says DON'T.** DNp19, DNp22, DNp102 and DNge107 were added as readouts (exported and verified) and the pooled signal was measured against its own noise in the decoder's OWN 150 ms window, 40 windows per condition:
| cell | rest L / R (Hz) | blank | L bright | R bright | separation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DNp20** | 35.0 / 44.5 | 0.000 +-0.276 | **-1.202 +-0.238** | **+1.372 +-0.213** | **+2.575** |
| DNge107 | 0.0 / 3.7 | 0.000 +-1.345 | +2.106 +-6.606 | +24.212 +-6.759 | +22.106 |
| DNp19 | 25.8 / 54.2 | 0.000 +-0.329 | -0.013 +-0.359 | +0.736 +-0.321 | +0.749 |
| DNp22 | 0.0 / 0.0 | 0.000 +-0.000 | -7.500 +-6.530 | +15.333 +-6.360 | +22.833 |
| DNp102 | 18.2 / 0.5 | 0.000 +-2.302 | -4.954 +-6.521 | -0.995 +-4.034 | +3.959 |

| decoding | separation | noise (sd at rest) | SNR | wrong-sign windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive pool of all five | +11.62 | 0.446 | 26.0x | **7/40 L-bright**, 0/40 R |
| **DNp20 alone** | +2.575 | 0.276 | **9.3x** | **0/40 and 0/40** |

**DNp20 alone is sign-perfect in BOTH directions; the pool is not.** The reason is normalisation: DNge107 and DNp22 rest at **0.0 Hz**, so dividing by their own resting rate is meaningless and a single burst becomes r = +24 with a sd of +-6.6. They are "silent or bursting" cells, not graded ones, and they swamp the pool. DNp19 barely separates (+0.749) and DNp102 has the wrong sign on both sides. So the pool is kept in the export as instrumentation, and **the decode uses DNp20 alone**.

**The earlier live failure was NOT DNp20's resolution.** Its raw 150 ms sd is 9.2 / 8.5 Hz - the quantisation I blamed - but normalised by its own 35 / 44.5 Hz resting rate, and with the two sides' noise partly cancelling in the difference, the blank sd is **0.276 against a +-1.3 signal**. The real blocker was the room: this ceiling is statically brighter on one side by ~0.05 in `oc`, larger than the +-0.04 a roll modulates.

**Proposed decoding (implemented, flag still off):**
`roll = clamp( ((DNp20_L - restL)/restL - (DNp20_R - restR)/restR ) / RIGHTING_FULL, -1, 1 )`, with `restL/restR` 8 s per-side running means and `RIGHTING_FULL` **1.3** = the measured one-sided response at ocelli 1.0. Pitch stays engineered: the common mode tracked the room, not the attitude (-0.44, -0.17, -0.40, +0.33 live).

**Room asymmetry, fixed in the lens.** A slow per-side baseline (`OCELLI_BASE_TAU_S` **60 s**) is subtracted from the ocelli OUTPUT, so the channel reports departure from whatever this room looks like rather than the room itself. **The cost, stated plainly: a roll held longer than ~60 s fades out of the signal.** The reflex therefore corrects DISTURBANCES and does not hold an absolute attitude - which is what an ocellar reflex does anyway, and the same trade the retina's tau and the decoder's 8 s habituation already make.

**The live loop, third attempt, and the blocker is the SCENE not the brain.** The constant forced roll could never test this - the 60 s baseline is seeded from the first sample, so a standing offset is zero by construction - so the seeder now FLIPS the bank every `RIGHTING_DEBUG_S` (6 s) and the telemetry prints which way it is rolled. Run 1, `OCELLI_FOV_RAD` 1.9 (109 deg): the ocelli L-R departure was **+-0.01**, occasionally 0.06, and did not track the bank sign at all. Diagnosis: a 109 deg field centred on the zenith sees ceiling in BOTH halves, and a uniform ceiling looks the same when you roll it. Real ocelli work off the sky/ground gradient at the HORIZON. Run 2 at **2.6 rad (149 deg)**, so each half straddles the horizon: the departure grew **7x to +-0.07** - the channel got much more sensitive - but it stayed a persistent +0.07 through both bank directions, and `rightRoll` was -0.30, +0.17, +0.19, +0.78, +0.08, -0.35, -0.21, +1.00, +0.10, -0.09: no correlation with the bank.

**Why, and it is not fixable by gain.** The fly is TRANSLATING through a partially scanned room while the test tries to read a rotation-induced gradient. Moving 20 cm changes what is overhead more than rolling 26 deg does, so the roll signal is buried under the translation signal. `OCELLI_GAIN` cannot separate them - it amplifies both equally, which is why the sweep was not run. This is the same reason real flies do not use ocelli for fast attitude: they use the HALTERES, which are inertial and immune to what the room looks like, and the ocelli only supplement them slowly.

**Decision: `RIGHTING_FROM_BRAIN` ships OFF and `BANK` / `PITCH_FWD` / `PITCH_HOVER` stay.** Closing a feedback loop on a signal that changes sign between telemetry windows would make the fly twitch at random. The code is in place and one flag away; what it needs is a disturbance the dorsal view can actually report - a stationary test, or an inertial sense (halteres) that the room cannot confound - not a different sign, not more cells and not more gain. **Decision: `BANK` / `PITCH_FWD` / `PITCH_HOVER` stay. No `RIGHTING_FROM_BRAIN` flag was added, and the lens-side upward camera was NOT built** - the gate was measured first precisely so that work would not be spent. This holds ADR 22's rule rather than breaking it: in this model motor-neuron rates are not a command surface.


## 71 — The first lesson: nine steps, a choice at every one, and a thing in your hand as the cue (15.09, Pavlo) — extends #66, #68, #70

Three asks in one feature, in the order Pavlo made them: *"a simple lesson … understood on the go"*, then *"so what is the final result: we teach the fly to land on the cup WHEN?"*, then *"in each instruction give the person options to choose"*.

**1. The end result, in one sentence.** **Pick up the cup and a trained fly comes and lands on it in your hand within seconds; a naive fly ignores it; a DANGER-trained fly flees when you lift it.** That is what the lesson teaches and what the TEST session measures. Everything below exists to make that sentence true and checkable.

**2. PRESENTING a thing replaces the abstract lure** (`FlySwarm.presentTick`, `Source.present`, `PRESENT_*`). A tracked palm within **15 cm** of a named thing for **0.5 s** turns that thing's own smell on at lure strength — its own glomerulus from ADR 65, plus the object drive — and the board says `SHOWING · CUP`. While it is held **the thing goes where the hand goes**, so the fly can land on it in your hand. When the hand leaves, the smell **fades over 3 s**, because a smell lingers. A plain `object` stays odourless until it is shown; only `food`/`scent` things keep a faint ambient smell, as before. ASK stays as the other way to present (voice). Nothing else is engineered: the fly still decides whether to come, and a refusal is still a MISS. Log: `PRESENT thing=cup by=hand` / `PRESENT_OFF`.

**3. The lesson is a wizard, not a script.** `mode: "lesson"` is FOOD/DANGER/TEST underneath, but it is driven by nine steps and **the person chooses at every one**. The strings and the tree live in `FlyTrainer` and travel in `TrainState.lesson` (`{step, of, title, line, action, options[], chosen}`), so the guide card and the web page render **the same words from the same source** and cannot drift. A choice arrives from a key on the card or from the page as `{t:"cmd", cmd:"lesson_choice", key}`; both go through `FlyTrainer.lessonChoice`, which logs `LESSON_CHOICE step=4 key=hand`.

| step | asks | choices |
|---|---|---|
| 1 MEET YOUR FLY | it has a real brain, nothing is scripted | LET'S GO |
| 2 WHAT TO TEACH IT | one thing can come to mean something | a thing means FOOD · a thing means DANGER · just TEST what it knows |
| 3 WHICH THING | the three nearest things the scan found | the room's own labels |
| 4 HOW TO SHOW IT | it has to notice the thing first | pick it up in your hand · say it (ASK) · put a treat on it — DANGER instead: rush a hand at it · a bitter treat |
| 5 HOW MUCH | more lessons, stronger memory, longer | quick 3 · thorough 6 |
| 6 MEASURE FIRST | the zero we compare to | (runs; "pick up the cup and count the seconds") |
| 7 THE LESSONS | n of N, the outcome arrives on landing | SKIP THE REST |
| 8 MEASURE AGAIN | the same test, and the control was never paid | SHOW ME THE VERDICT |
| 9 THE VERDICT | Gemini's plain words | teach the opposite · make it forget · save this brain · done |

"Just test" skips 4 and 5 and runs the TEST plan (plasticity off for the session, ADR 66). **TEACH THE FLY opens straight into the lesson when nothing has ever been taught** (`history()` empty and `memory.changed === 0`); otherwise it opens the picker, whose first key — wide, above the 2x4 grid — is `FIRST LESSON · 5 MIN`.

**4. The card.** The lesson owns the whole card: a big step number, the title, one line, one action, and at most three choice keys in the picker's own style. ## 72 — PROPOSAL, not a change: how you could teach the fly a TRICK, and what each way lets us claim (15.09, Pavlo asked what others do)

Seven public projects were read end to end (read outside this repo, nothing of them in it). **Not one of them has shown a connectome learning a trick from a biological rule.** The field splits cleanly, and the split is the whole decision.

**What others actually do.**

| project | what is trained | rule | result | control that decides it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `eudald-seeslab/train-your-fly` | **15.09 M per-edge gains** (a multiplier layer; the synapse counts stay a frozen buffer) + a linear KC readout | AdamW + cross-entropy through 3 message passes | colour ~100 %, shape ~64 %, number ~71 % (chance 50) | **randomised connectomes BEAT the biological one** (number 83 vs 71, shape 70 vs 64). Their own paper: *"structure alone supports efficient visual computation"* |
| `nftechie/doomfly` (our own lineage's sibling) | 4,184 real KC->MBON11 synapses, in place | the SAME centred anti-Hebbian rule we run | *"Status: not demonstrated learned survival."* | **the unpaired control changed identically** (suppression 0.9379/0.0488 in both arms); timing-shuffled changed as many synapses; **frozen survived LONGER** (5.83 s vs 3.657 s); blind beat sighted |
| `jonatasperaza/FlyPong` | 404 real `object/target->descending` synapses, in place | three-factor dopamine (PAM/PPL1), LR 0.02 | **no learning in any variant**; the isolated-channel test favoured the FROZEN control 5/6 | a trivial oracle AND a matched delayed population-code baseline both hit **1.0 in every seed**; fixed weight 40 gives 0.994 where the rule could never reach past 11.9 |
| `lixiang1076/fly-brain` | a JSON multiplier table over KC->MBON | multiply by 0.3 / 1.5 on a chat keyword | no tests, no baseline, no number | — |
| `liuzihe02/fly-craftax` | **READOUT**: 1,314 DN rates -> 7 actions (9,205 params) | PPO, 76.8 k steps, 58.6 min on a 4090 | achievements 21 vs 9 zero-shot | **black vision scores the SAME** (27 vs 25 achievements) and an open-loop clock beats it: *"not a visually guided policy"* |
| `cobanov/flyjump` | **READOUT**: 16 DN activities -> 12 -> 3 (**243 params**) | CEM, pop 64, 80 generations, **7.6 minutes in JavaScript** | **99/100 held-out courses** vs 0/100 for silenced, untrained, random, idle and an untuned rule | honest: *"demonstrates learned control and dependence on circuit activity, NOT superiority of biological topology"* — and its "silenced" row is numerically identical to idle, so it only proves the readout needs some time-varying input |
| `seanphan/flyt3` | READOUT (10,250 params) by self-play REINFORCE; a second path depresses real KC->MBON | REINFORCE; dopamine-RPE depression | 62 % vs random (readout), 74 % (MB path) | random opponent only; no metrics committed; its own side-experiment found a **plain pixel MLP beat the circuit 85.7 % vs 60.8 %** |

**doomfly is the one to read twice.** It is the same connectome, the same rule and the same US as ours, and it failed on exactly the hazard ADR 65 hit and ADR 67 resolved: **the unpaired control moved identically.** Their answer was to declare the assay failed. Ours was to stop averaging — `memory.eff_on` splits the synapses by which cue drives them, and the effect appears (-0.010 to -0.020 against the control, three sessions, frozen control exactly 0.000). That is a real difference in method, and it is the strongest thing we have.

**The three honest options for "teach it a trick" (a loop, a somersault, a clap-cue).**

**(1) Real KC->MBON valence — what we already have.** Cost: zero, it ships. What a user can do: teach a THING to mean food or danger and measure it. What it can never do: a loop or a somersault — the rule changes a valence, not a motor program, and there is no path from MBON to "do a barrel roll" in this connectome. Claim it allows, and it is a strong one: *"a real memory forms on the real synapses of a reconstructed brain, measured against an unpaired control and a frozen control."*

**(2) A learned READOUT — `FlyBody` learns to read the brain. RECOMMENDED.** Train a small policy from descending-neuron rates to our action map, with the reward given **by the user in AR** (a pinch, a treat, "good fly"). `flyjump` is the proof of feasibility: **243 parameters, CEM, 7.6 minutes, 99/100** — that is a phone-sized budget, not a GPU cluster. Cost: a few hundred parameters, an on-device or on-page CEM loop, one new disclosed module; the brain is **byte-identical throughout**. What a user can do: teach a trick with a name — a loop on a clap, a landing on a raised hand — and see it work. What it must ship with, non-negotiable, because two of the three readout projects failed exactly here: **the ablation control** — silence or shuffle the brain's contribution and show the trick collapses, against a *time-varying* brain-free input, not a constant one (flyjump's silenced row equalled idle, which proves nothing). Claim it allows: *"the body learned to read the brain; the brain did not change"* — and, if the ablation holds, *"and the trick depends on the brain's own activity."* Never *"the fly learned a trick."*
**(3) Trained per-synapse gains inside the connectome (`train-your-fly` style).** Cost: highest — 15 M parameters, backprop, a GPU, and a fitted brain that is no longer the reconstruction. What a user can do: in principle anything the gradient can reach. What it costs us is the whole premise: **their own randomised-wiring control beats the biological graph**, so the moment we fit gains we can no longer say the behaviour comes from the connectome. Exact-by-default off, and only ever as a labelled experiment beside the real brain.

**Recommendation: (2), with (1) kept running underneath.** (1) is the science and it stays the headline; (2) is the trick, and it is the only route anyone has made work inside a five-minute training budget. They do not conflict — one changes the brain's synapses, the other changes how the body reads an unchanged brain, and the honest sentence for each is different and both are true. **(3) is not worth its price.**

**If (2) is taken up, the gates before it may be demonstrated:** the ablation control above; a matched brain-free baseline with the same parameter count; the training budget and seed count published with the result; and the readout's inputs named (which DNs, how many), because "reads the brain" is only meaningful if the list is small enough to check.

## 73 — The halteres are a rotation MAGNITUDE sense in this model, not a yaw direction (15.09) — closes the backlog "haltere channel from yaw rate", extends #70
**The backlog asked for a haltere channel "from yaw rate". Measured, the direction is not there and the magnitude is - and what the magnitude DOES is exactly what halteres are for.**

**Anatomy, and it is far better than the ocelli (#70).** MaleCNS has **205 haltere afferents**, every one entering through **DMetaN**, the dorsal metathoracic (haltere) nerve, split **104 left / 101 right by rootSide**, with **26,638 outgoing edges, 100 % of them excitatory** (mean weight 1.16). Unlike the ocellar bottleneck this is a BROAD projection:
| haltere afferents -> | sum weight | edges |
|---|---|---|
| neck motor neurons | **+998.8** | 603 |
| wing motor neurons | +1007.6 | 524 |
| descending neurons | +1040.9 | 1108 |
(the ocellar OCG01b managed +302.8 over 14 edges). Top single targets are ascending neurons and VNC interneurons - AN08B010 +700, IN01A031 +637, ANXXX136 +483, w-cHIN +320 - plus `b3 MN` +187 (a wing steering muscle) and `CvN5` +186 (a neck MN). **Note what is NOT there: zero edges to DNa02, DNp15, DNp20, DNg02, DNp09 or MDN.** The haltere pathway climbs through ANs, it does not touch the descending cells we read.

**The sense.** `"haltere": (r"^", "root", "haltere")` - all 205, sided by rootSide. The receptors are all present; what the model lacks is the BEATING, so as with the eye (#54) and the ocelli (#70) we supply what the mechanics would supply: a rate-proportional drive per side. Disclosed, same pattern.

**Gate 1 - DIRECTION (differential, the yaw encoding the backlog asked for): FAILS.** L = base+d, R = base-d, 40 windows of 150 ms per point:
| readout | separation (R+ vs L+) at d=0.45 | noise | SNR | wrong-sign windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| neck | -0.302 | 0.192 | 1.6x | **32/40 and 31/40** |
| steermn | -0.261 | 0.069 | 3.8x | 16/40 and **40/40** |
| power | -0.015 | 0.030 | 0.5x | 24/40 and 28/40 |
| DNa02 | -1.066 | 2.133 | 0.5x | 27/40 and 21/40 |
Nothing separates by side. `steermn` moves monotonically but in the SAME direction for both drives - a common-mode response wearing a differential's clothes. **The breadth that made the anatomy look promising is exactly what washes the laterality out:** 205 afferents projecting over 26,638 edges into shared VNC interneurons do not preserve a left-right difference.

**Gate 2 - MAGNITUDE (common mode, L = R = v): PASSES, cleanly.**
| v | neck_L | neck_R | steermn_L | power_L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | +0.003 +-0.18 | +0.028 +-0.15 | -0.012 +-0.09 | +0.014 +-0.10 |
| 0.25 | +0.003 +-0.18 | +0.028 +-0.15 | -0.012 +-0.09 | +0.014 +-0.10 |
| 0.50 | +0.081 +-0.20 | +0.136 +-0.17 | -0.127 +-0.07 | -0.088 +-0.12 |
| 0.75 | +0.238 +-0.23 | +0.254 +-0.18 | -0.278 +-0.07 | -0.486 +-0.10 |
| **1.00** | **+0.371 +-0.17** | **+0.300 +-0.15** | -0.246 +-0.07 | **-0.517 +-0.05** |
Monotone on every row, threshold between 0.25 and 0.50 (v = 0.25 is bit-identical to rest - ADR 31's ~7 mV floor again), SNR at v = 1.0: **power 10.3x**, neck_L 2.2x, neck_R 2.0x, steermn 3.1x.

**And the direction of the effect is the biology.** More haltere drive -> **power motor neurons DOWN (-0.52) and neck motor neurons UP (+0.37)**: *"I am rotating fast -> cut wing power, stiffen the neck."* That is flight stabilisation and gaze stabilisation, which is what halteres are for. **It also moves the neck pool, which the ocelli could never do** (#70: 10.8 Hz flat under every stimulus).

**Addendum (15.09): there is NO engineered damping term for it to replace. Audited, line by line.** The question was which constant in `FlyBody` means "slow down / cut power / stiffen when rotating fast". Every candidate, and why none of them is one:
| term | where | what it actually is | rotation-rate dependent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SACC_CRUISE_TURN` **0.5** | FlyBody L513 | smooth steering runs at half `TURN_RATE`, and at **0** while a saccade is running | **no** - event-gated on `saccT > 0`, and it damps STEERING, not power |
| `TURN_RATE` **1.5** rad/s | L513 | the steering gain at steer = +-1 | no - a fixed gain |
| `SPEED_EASE_RATE` **2.5** (escape 12) | L527 | a first-order lag so the brain's stop cannot flip speed 0 <-> cruise in one frame | no - a time constant, not a rate term |
| `speed = act.forward * CRUISE_CM_S` | L411 | speed IS the brain's `forward` (thrust - stop - escape) | **no rotation term at all** |
| `buzzT` | L519 | take-off buzz holds speed at 0 | no - event-gated |
| `nearFly` brake, approach brake | L119, L413-417 | slow down near another fly / holding time-to-contact | no - distance-based |
The only thing in the body that suppresses anything during a rotation is `SACC_CRUISE_TURN` going to zero **while a saccade runs**, and that is a binary event gate on steering, not a rate-proportional cut of power. **Nothing anywhere reads a yaw or roll rate and reduces thrust or stiffens the neck because of it.**

**So the swap has no target, and by the gate rule the work stops here: no `DAMPING_FROM_BRAIN` flag, no export, no restart.** The haltere channel stays what it measurably is - a rotation-magnitude sense with a clean, monotone, 10x-SNR effect on the power motor neurons, waiting for a behaviour that wants it.

**One thing worth knowing for whoever does want it:** the rate it needs already exists and is exact. `FlySwarm` L2053-2054 already computes the per-fly yaw rate `wz`, with proper angle unwrapping, to compensate the `motion` channel for the fly's own rotation. A haltere drive of `v = floor + k*(|wz| + |roll rate|)` is a few lines on top of a number the lens already has - the missing piece was never the sense, it is that the body has no damping behaviour to hand over.

**Decision: stop at the measurement, as the gate rule requires.** The directional signal fails, so no `RIGHTING_FROM_BRAIN` swap and no export/restart was spent. The `haltere` SENSES entry is left in `channels.py` (uncommitted, absent from every exported `.flyb`, so nothing in flight is affected) for whoever takes the next step. **What it would replace is not the roll constant but a damping term** - the honest next question is which engineered term in `FlyBody` currently does "slow down when rotating fast", and whether this can take it over.

## 68 — Teaching lives on the web brain; the page drives it and Gemini reads the result (15.09, Pavlo) — extends #62, #65, #66, #67, #63

**Why.** On the glasses alone one 50 ms brain step costs 1-2 s of the user's (ADR 50), so a six-bout
session is an hour of standing still and every probe reads a fly that has barely thought — numbers
that look like data and are not. The page runs the same brain in ~100 ms a slice instead of seconds (ADR 55/86), it is where a
person can actually see the rows, and it is where the versions and the history live. So teaching is a
web-brain feature, said out loud instead of silently degrading on the glasses.

**1. One gate.** `FlyTrainer.available()` = `!TRAIN_WEB_ONLY || host.pageOn()`. The board's TEACH key
**stays visible** — it is how a person learns the feature exists — but dim, with the reason in its own
hint line (`FlyBoard.setTrainAvailable(ready, why)`, diff-cached, no new draw call): *"TEACHING NEEDS
THE WEB BRAIN // open the page, PIN 4821"*. The press is refused, the picker never opens, and every
entry point (`setOn`, `choose`, `command`) logs `TRAIN_UNAVAILABLE reason=native`. The guide's step 03
line becomes *"PAGE CONNECTED // teaching available"* the moment `webActive` is true.

**2. The page drives a session.** A `cmd` event on the relay socket the brain stream already uses, so
a page that never sends one behaves exactly as before. `WebBrainLink.onCommand` -> `FlySwarm.pageCommand`
-> `FlyTrainer.command()`. The guide's picker and the page go through **the same code**, so a session
started from a laptop is the same session, logged the same way.

| packet | what the lens does |
|---|---|
| `{t:"cmd", cmd:"train", mode, cs, csMinus?, bouts?}` | starts that session; `cs`/`csMinus` are matched against the room's real labels and refused (logged) if unknown, so the page can only name something the fly can find |
| `{t:"cmd", cmd:"train_stop"}` | abandons whatever is running |

Feed additions, each sent only when it changes: **`things`** (the exact labels `command` matches a cue

**3. Gemini reads the result, and may not invent a number.** At `TRAIN_DONE` the trainer builds an

## 74 — The page brain draws by a detail ladder with an auto controller, and a spike is a front leaving the soma (15.09) — extends #55, #61

Sixteen candidates, from 0.2 % to 70 % of edges removed, were measured against the full brain (5 s of brain time each, 1,600 hops, Metal kernel, harness verified bit-deterministic: the same run twice differs by 0.0). **Not one meets the gate** (every named readout within 10 % rate error and DNa02 steering sign agreement above 95 %). Nothing was exported. Method, tables and blind spots: `core/brain_prune/LITE.md`; harness `core/brain_prune/lite.py`.
- **The number that settles it.** The gentlest candidate drops 56,671 edges — **0.22 % of the edges carrying 0.046 % of the total |w|** — and scores **worse** on steering sign (74.0 %) than cuts three hundred times larger. Across the whole table the error is flat: 8-35 % mean rate error, 74-83 % sign agreement, from a 0.2 % cut to a 70 % cut. **There is no fidelity-versus-size curve.** #47 concluded pruning breaks behaviour and blamed the 16/28/46 % of weight that the >=3/5/10-contact cuts removed; that reason is wrong. Losing 0.046 % does it too. What we are seeing is a chaotic trajectory: the network sits at a bistable resting point and any perturbation decorrelates it within about 2 s.
- **What that means beyond pruning, and it is the important part.** A single run's behaviour is only ~75-80 % reproducible under *any* perturbation of the network. So every behaviour metric we read off one session carries that noise floor — including the training verdicts (#67, #71). This is the mechanism behind the honest `UNCLEAR` the page prints: the synapses move reliably and measurably, the *turn* does not follow reliably in one session. Any future behavioural claim needs repeats and an ensemble, not a single trajectory. The brain is not broken; it is chaotic, like the animal.
- **Three tiers of readout, measured.** Prune-proof at every cut (<=3.5 % error): escape DNp01/02/04/11, LC4, LPLC2, PAM11 — a loom injects 20 mV directly and drives 130-266 Hz, which swamps anything a prune changes; escape events were 59-60 of 60 in every candidate and 0 of 60 false escapes anywhere. Drifting 9-60 %: DNa02, DNp66, power MNs, MDN, PPL101 — the shape stays right (corr 0.89-1.00) but the level slides, and our decoder reads a difference from a habituating baseline, so a 25 % drift kills the turn (full brain turns -0.67 toward food on the left; the 33 %-of-edges candidate turns -0.09). Broken at any cut: MN9 `feed`, DNpe007 `stop`, MBON07 — these are 1 to 4 cells at a few Hz, i.e. threshold-crossing devices; `feed` inverts outright and `stop` goes 0.00 -> 0.78 in neutral (a fly frozen on the spot). Rescaling the survivors to compensate for lost drive does not help and at one candidate blows MBON07 up 46x.
- **The one lead, and why it is not evidence.** 131,399 of 166,700 neurons (78.8 %) never fired in the whole battery and own 68.9 % of the out-edges. Dropping a cell's out-edges is exactly behaviour-preserving for a run in which it never fires — but the battery that defines the silent set is the battery that scores it, so the result is circular. It is weaker still than it looks: **no scenario in the battery ever sent an `eye` payload**, so T4/T5/HS/VS read 0.0 Hz throughout and the optic lobe — 104,702 cells, **62.8 % of the brain** — was dark by construction. Most of that 78.8 % is an untested input, not a dormant cell. To become real this needs a definition battery with the compound eye actually driven, an independent validation battery from the glasses' own `dbg` sense traces, and an ADR for cells that wake up later.
- **Where the device speed comes from instead:** the kernel (fixed-point accumulation so the GPU kernel passes its own exactness check, and i16 contact weights: the edge payload is bit-exactly halvable because |w|/0.275 is integral for 100.000000 % of the 25.1 M edges, max 2591) and the lens frames (#80), which is what lets the load policy give the brain its duty back.
- Tooling left behind: `export.py` takes `--mask <bool .npy over the original edge order>` next to `--cmin`; it has never been exercised end to end, because no export was worth running.

## 82 — The GPU kernel is exact because the arithmetic cannot differ, not because the orders match (16.09) — extends #52, #55; the numbers in #81 are its licence
- **Why.** The GPU kernel failed its own self-check (`gpu_check`: cells_diff 85,673, spikes 103,280 vs 103,185 after three chunks), so a 4x faster kernel sat unused. The hypothesis on the table was that the synaptic scatter's float adds ran in a different order on the GPU. **Measured first: that was not it** — the old kernel reproduced the CPU's delivery order exactly (per-target linked list + insertion sort) and was `cells_diff 0` on MoltenVK at 3 chunks. What it actually was: the kernel's exactness rested on two things nothing guarantees.
- **The two.** (1) `evolve` was one long float expression and the shader hard-coded the `fma` sequence clang was *assumed* to contract it into, plus a two-fma emulation of its two divisions — which is only correct if the GPU's `fma` is truly fused, and several mobile GPUs lower it onto an unfused `mad`. (2) the synaptic current was a float `+=` per delivery, so the sum depended on the order, and the GPU spent a linked list, a per-target sort, a 64 MB record buffer and an O(k^2) fallback rebuilding that order.
- **The proof it was marginal everywhere, not just on one GPU:** the pre-16.09 kernel **fails its own check on the Mac** once the run is long enough — 3 chunks ok, **10 chunks `cells_diff` 154,678 and spikes 750,132 vs 750,816**, 20 chunks worse. We had only ever run three.
- **What now.** `evolve` uses nothing but `+`, `-` and `*` (correctly rounded by IEEE-754 and by the Vulkan spec on every implementation), with `#pragma clang fp contract(off)` on the CPU and `precise` in the shader so neither side may invent an `fma`; the two divisions are pre-computed into two new decay tables (`(a-b)/3`, `tau/(tau-20)(cc-a)`), built **once** by the core and handed to both backends. And the synaptic weight is stored as the **integer it already is**: the connectome's weight is `sign x 0.275 mV x contacts` and `|w|/0.275` is a whole number for **100.000000 %** of the 25,147,397 edges (1..2591 contacts, 788 distinct values). A tick's deliveries into one cell are summed with `atomicAdd` / a stamped per-target accumulator and enter `g` with ONE float rounding. Integer addition is associative, so there is no order left to reproduce.
- **What that deletes.** The record buffer (64 MB), the sort scratch (16 MB), the per-target linked list, the insertion sort, the O(k^2) fallback and **both** overflow failure modes (`rcap`, `scap`); the decay table went 2.1 MB -> 160 KB. About **79 MB less GPU memory**, and one fewer thing that can fail silently under load.
- **Measured (M1 Max, MoltenVK, `brain_c0`).** New kernel: `cells_diff 0` at 3, 10 and 20 chunks, in both arithmetic modes. Speed on the same state, 10 chunks: CPU 372 -> 382-390 ms (+3-5 %), GPU 231 -> 216-222 ms (-5 %). The fixed-point scatter is not slower; it is slightly faster on the GPU and pays for itself in memory.
- **The file, FLYB v2.** `export.py` writes `wq` (i16 signed contacts) + `w_quantum` (f64) instead of `weight` (f32) and refuses rather than rounding if a weight is off the grid; the core reads v1 and v2, so every existing export still loads. `tools/flyb_quantise.py` converts one without re-running the exporter. **206.6 -> 156.3 MB** uncompressed; the `.z` the page downloads only **77.0 -> 72.1 MB**, because zlib already exploited the 788 distinct float values — the honest number, not the halving the raw arithmetic suggests. RAM is unchanged (edges stay 8 bytes: the plastic weights need sub-contact resolution, so units are i32). **`post` stays i32**: only 55.8 % of targets are under 65,536 after the hot reorder, so a u16/u32 split would save under half of the other 100.6 MB at the price of a misaligned `Edge` and a branch in the hottest loop.
- **The cost, disclosed.** Each tick's membrane update is about one ULP away from the pre-16.09 kernel, and the synaptic sum is *more* accurate (one rounding instead of ~150). Measured: the two are the same run for the first ~100 ms of simulated time and diverge exponentially after — 150 ms: 8 spikes apart, 300 ms: 562, 500 ms: 16 %. That is exactly the noise floor **#81** measured and named: this brain is chaotic and any perturbation decorrelates it within about 2 s. So this is not a new kind of uncertainty, it is the one we already live with — but it does mean a behaviour battery, not a diff, is what says the fly still behaves. One consequence worth keeping: with the float scatter the two arithmetic modes landed in *different activity regimes* (39 k vs 60 k spikes a step); with the integer scatter they land in the same one. Summing ~150 float deliveries one at a time was losing enough to matter.
- **A/B without a rebuild.** `{"acc":"plain"}` (default) and `{"acc":"fma"}` switch **both** kernels together; `fma` is the pre-16.09 arithmetic bit for bit (verified against the old build, step for step). The check line now carries `"acc"`, `"wk"`, `"wexact"` so a build is identifiable from a log, plus `dv`/`dg`/`da`/`dlast`/`drefr` and the worst ULP per field: `dv` alone at 1 ULP is an evaluation difference, `dg` at a large ULP is a lost or doubled delivery, `dlast`/`drefr` is a logic bug.
- **There is no RNG in this model** (grepped: no `rand`/`random`/`noise`/`seed` in the core or any kernel), so the "hash the noise per (seed, step, neuron)" half of the brief had nothing to fix.
- Full spot-by-spot note with file:line: `core/DETERMINISM.md`.
- **Resident memory, found while in here (16.09, Pavlo asked).** The core does **not** hold the connectome twice — `set_gpu` frees the CPU edge arrays once the Vulkan buffer owns them and the CPU kernel reads the mapped GPU buffer (ADR 52's design, preserved). But the process *showed* both, because an allocator keeps large freed blocks on its free list and a memory-pressure killer looks at resident size. Fixed with a `release_memory()` (`malloc_zone_pressure_relief` / `malloc_trim` / `mallopt(M_PURGE)`) after load, after the GPU takes the edges and after warm-up; a v2 file's contacts are never widened into a temporary f32 array; and the GPU's edge array is filled one source array at a time. Measured on the Mac, `brain_c0` v2: resident **880.1 -> 268.6 MB** with the GPU up, **630.6 -> 211.6 MB** CPU-only. **Left open:** the bring-up PEAK is still ~730 MB. It is load-time temporaries, not a second connectome; the fix is SoA edge buffers (`post` and `units` as two buffers instead of one interleaved `Edge`) filled straight from the FLYB blob, which already stores both as separate contiguous arrays.

## 85 — No data until there IS data: the boot card, and the brain line in words (17.09, Pavlo) — extends #78, #58, #50/#52, #82

- **The complaint, exactly.** On the glasses the first one to three minutes are a dashboard of zeros, `BRAIN OFFLINE`, no fly, and visible lag, with nothing explaining any of it: *"поки не зрозуміло чи мозок розгортається чи ні"*, *"не показувати дані поки мозок розгортається взагалі"*, *"це візуальний процес який треба буде показати щоб пояснити чому так лагає"*.
- **Why the zeros are worse than nothing.** #78 withholds the fly until a brain steps it — correctly — but the board was still drawing NEURAL / BODY rows at 0.0 Hz for a brain that did not exist yet. A measurement panel reading zero says "measured zero", not "not measured". That is the same class of lie the ADRs forbid in the fly's motion, made in the UI instead.
- **Call: the dashboard is not what comes after DONE SCANNING.** A third card is (`FlyBoot.ts`), in the same flow and the same language as the start and scan cards (#58): six stages, each pending / running / done / failed / skipped, with a lamp, the row's own result, a sweep on whichever row is working, and one live number. The dashboard pops in under ITS tail, the moment the first `BrainMsg` lands.
  1 SESSION · 2 THE ROOM · 3 THE BRAIN FILE (77 MB) · 4 UNPACKING + WARM-UP · 5 CHECKING THE GPU · 6 THE FIRST THOUGHT.
- **Every stage is read, never assumed.** The card derives all six from state that already existed and was already public — `NativeBrainLink.status` ("loading" / "fetching brain" / "native" / "socket (<reason>)"), `gpuDevice`, `gpuLost`, `gpuCheck`, and the arrival of a `BrainMsg`. **No new state and no change to NativeBrainLink.** The stage clocks tick from the lens's first frame, not from DONE SCANNING, because the download starts at boot.
- **There is no download progress bar, and there cannot be one — checked, not assumed.** `InternetModule.fetch` returns a `Response` whose only body accessors are `text()` / `bytes()` / `json()` / `blob()`, each resolving with the WHOLE body; `Support/StudioLib.d.ts` has no `body`, no `ReadableStream`, no `getReader` and no progress callback anywhere (the only `onProgress` in the whole d.ts belongs to `instantiateAsync`). So the running row shows **elapsed seconds plus the range measured so far**, and the total only after the fact ("77 MB in 62 s"). A percentage here would have been invented. *(The one honest way to get real bytes would be to fetch the file as N `Range:` requests and concatenate — more code, more failure modes, and it changes the boot path; not taken.)*
- **Stage 5 is the answer to "why does it lag".** The ~70 s stutter is the exactness self-check of #82: both kernels run the same brain so they can be compared cell for cell. The card says so before it happens ("it stutters while it compares") and the board's brain line says it while it happens. The signal is `gpuDevice` set and `gpuCheck` still empty — the core asks for the check on the same `ready` message that names the device.
- **The brain line, rewritten (Pavlo: "Brain native x1 25% — треба розділити якось і пояснити що це за 25%").** `x1` was kernel threads and `25%` the duty the load policy allows; neither is actionable by a wearer. Split three ways:
  - the header keeps only whether there IS a brain — `BRAIN LIVE` / `BRAIN WAKING` / `BRAIN ON A PAGE` / `BRAIN OFFLINE` / `NO BRAIN`;
  - a strip in the right column (the slot the retired one-fly tabs left free) says **where** it runs (`IN THE GLASSES · GPU`), **how fast** it thinks (`N s / step`) and what that means — `50 ms of fly time per step · Nx slower than you` — or, when it is held back, **why in words**: the lens needs the frames / the lens needs the CPU / the device is busy / resting, nobody is near it / a page took over. The raw policy strings are mapped, never shown;
  - the raw numbers (level, kernel, threads, duty, step) move to the **"?" help card**, one dim mono line under its live line. They are read through read-only getters on `BrainLoad` (`uiLevel`/`uiDuty`/`uiThreads`/`uiRest`/`uiWhy`) — windows only, nothing that can steer the policy.
- **No Wi-Fi is an answer, not a spinner.** `brain fetch HTTP 0` marks stage 3 FAILED, the card says `NO WI-FI · the download never started`, the foot of the card says what to do ("connect the glasses to Wi-Fi in the Spectacles app, then start again"), and `OPEN THE BOARD ANYWAY` appears. The key opens the dashboard; it does **not** produce a fly — #78 still owns that. A 25 s watchdog does the same when there is no in-lens core coming at all (the editor preview, `NATIVE_ENABLED` off) and nothing answers over the socket either, so the card can never spin forever.
- **Cost, disclosed.** +2 draw calls while the boot card is up (its own quad batch and its own text mesh), then zero — the card's root is disabled and it is never ticked again once the dashboard opens. The brain strip adds 2 quads to the board's existing batch (no draw call) and **3 Component.Texts, i.e. +3 draw calls whenever the board is in view**. They are written at 6 Hz and diff-cached. If the board's draw budget gets tight again, the `where` line is the one that changes rarely enough to be folded into a rebuildable text mesh.
- **Editor limits.** The preview has no brain of its own, so stages 3-5 there read SKIPPED and only the glasses can show them. Verified in the editor: the lens compiles and boots, the start and scan cards are unchanged, and the boot card's object tree is built (`FlyBoot` / `BootNeon` / `BootText` / `BootAnyway`) without throwing.
- **The page's WASM build flags, measured and not adopted (17.09).** `-msimd128` and `-flto` were proposed on the assumption that the page is CPU-bound. **It is not:** the phase table on the page's real engine says `{"gpu":68.4,"busy":0,"cpu":0,"sense":0.9,"plast":0.2,"read":0.1,"msg":0,"gc":5,"cc":0,"step":71.2}` — every chunk on the GPU and **the whole WASM CPU side is 2.8 ms of a 71.2 ms hop, 4 %**. Measured anyway — three INTERLEAVED sets of the 1300 ms warm-up in node on an M1 Max, each vs `-O3` in the same set: **`-msimd128` +1.0 % and −0.1 %** (nothing, 309 KB), **`-flto` +2.1 %** (340 KB), **`-msimd128 -flto` +2.1 / +1.6 / +2.5 %** (344 KB). So the flag that pays is **`-flto`, about 2 % for 36 KB**, and SIMD buys nothing — which is what you expect when the kernel is a random-access scatter over a 200 MB edge array: there is nothing there to vectorise. Kept at `-O3` anyway: 2 % is 2 % of the WASM CPU side, which is 4 % of a hop, i.e. 0.08 % end to end, and only for a visitor with no WebGPU at all. `OPT=`/`OUT=` hooks in `build_wasm.sh` make the A/B one env var. Reach was never the objection: WASM SIMD is baseline wherever WebGPU is, Windows/D3D12 included. **A method note worth more than the result, and it caught me twice:** the first A/B said SIMD was 30 % SLOWER (three headless Chromes competing for the machine), and the second credited SIMD with 1.5 % that an attribution run showed was LTO's, in the same build. Interleave the variants, repeat the SET, and change one flag at a time.
- **A free proof of ADR 82 fell out of it:** all three WASM builds and the native CPU produce the **same spike counts, exactly** (64315 / 60548 / 65370). Before this checkpoint the RUNBOOK said a WASM-vs-native difference was expected "because the WASM CPU has no fma" — removing the kernel's dependence on compiler-chosen fma removed that too. WASM is ~3.1x slower than native per hop (610 vs 198 ms), which is now the only difference between them.
- **Threads on the page are not a build flag and are not enabled.** `-pthread -sPTHREAD_POOL_SIZE=4` does compile (one warning: `-pthread + ALLOW_MEMORY_GROWTH may run non-wasm code slowly`), but shipping it needs cross-origin isolation (`COOP: same-origin` + `COEP: require-corp`, and the page is `crossOriginIsolated=false` today — verified), which then blocks every cross-origin subresource that does not opt in, and this page talks to Supabase; a thread POOL rather than the per-chunk `std::thread` spawns `kernel_par` does today; and it must survive Asyncify, which the module needs for WebGPU readback and which emscripten warns about combining with blocking joins. Details in `web/DEPLOY.md`. And it would only speed up the CPU fallback — 4 % of a hop on the engine the page actually uses.

## ADR 86 — The in-lens brain is paused, and the page is the only brain (17.09)

**Status:** accepted. **Superseded by #87**, which deleted what this ADR only switched off.

- **The call.** The glasses no longer carry a brain: they are senses, motion and a display, and a web page holding the PIN is the only thing that can think for a fly.
- **Why now.** The glasses became unavailable, so nothing in the in-lens path could be verified any more, and an unverifiable path that is ON is worse than one that is off. The engineering case had already arrived at the same place: the in-lens brain was *correct* (ADR 82) but far slower than a page's 50-750 ms per hop. That is not a slow experience, it is a different one.
- **What being off buys.** No brain download at every launch and no self-check stutter on the glasses. With the LAN socket already off (ADR 84) and `EXPERIMENTAL_API` gone, the lens is **publishable**.
- **What it costs, said plainly.** The lens is no longer autonomous. With no page, ADR 78 withholds the fly and the boot card says why; its stuck-advice names the page instead of `scripts/run_server.sh` (unreachable with the socket off).

## ADR 87 — The in-lens brain path is deleted, and what stays behind (20.09)

**Status:** accepted. **Supersedes #86**, which switched the in-lens brain off but kept it.

- **The call.** Pavlo, 20.09: "вирізаємо повністю, цей шлях не актуальний". Gone: the in-lens brain and everything that served only it — `NativeBrainLink.ts`, `BrainLoad.ts`, `TeachGatedNativeScenario.ts`, their scripts, and 31 dead `NATIVE_` config keys.

The two `WEB_NATIVE_` ones keep their names because they govern the brain the **Mac** runs, which runs `server.py --engine native`.
- **What this closes.** ADR 50/51/52/83's load policy, rest and startle burst are now history, not code — the thing they governed is gone. ADR 82's exactness work is NOT history: it still guards the WebGPU kernel every build. ADR 86's parked question is closed; nothing in the lens runs a brain any more.

The card is **SESSION · THE ROOM · THE BRAIN** and nothing reads SKIPPED. What is genuinely left over is small and wants a non-regex tidy-up: three dead helpers in `FlyBoot.ts` (`gpuWords`, `socketReason`, `gpuCheckRunning` — the last still read at `FlyBoot.ts:148`, but it can never be true without a native link) and `FlyBoard.brainLoad`, which `FlySwarm` no longer assigns.

## ADR 88 — An appetitive session starts by making the fly hungry (21.09)

**Status:** accepted. A disclosed override, in the sense the hard rule means: the trainer sets the animal's internal state, it does not move the animal.

- **The measurement that forced it.** A full FOOD session ran to completion under LEAF against a real page brain: 582 s, 16 trial rows, the protocol's own `TRAIN_DONE why=done`. Every single latency was `MISS` and `us16_rows=0` — not one trial delivered its 16 unconditioned-stimulus pulses. With no US there is no pairing, and with no pairing there is nothing for the mushroom body to learn. The session was perfect and empty.
- **Why.** `FOOD_HUNGRY` is 0.6: a fly looks for food only below 60 % energy. Nothing in `FlyTrainer` had ever touched energy, so a session begun on a fed fly asked it to fly to a smell it had no reason to want. Appetitive conditioning on a sated animal is not a weak experiment, it is no experiment — which is why a real protocol food-deprives the fly before one.
- **The call.** `TrainHost.starve(fly)` is new, `FlySwarm` implements it as `energy = min(energy, FOOD_HUNGRY * 0.7)`, and `FlyTrainer` calls it when an appetitive mode starts (`food`, `lesson`, `choice`, `test`, `transfer`), logging `TRAIN_STARVE`. Comfortably under the threshold, so the fly is foraging from the first trial rather than hovering at it.
- **Why this is not the thing ADR 22/24/26 forbid.** Those forbid faking the fly's BEHAVIOUR — moving it, or making it look as if the brain decided something it did not. This sets hunger, which is an input to the brain like any other sense, and the brain still decides what to do about it. It is logged, it is here, and the guide can say it.
- **Found on the way, not fixed here.** `WorldSources` gives class `object` sigma 0 and strength 0 — a thing in the room has no smell of its own, and only borrows the lure's smell while a person is HOLDING it (`present > 0`, ADR 70). A headless test therefore has to stand in for the wearer's hand, which `CyberFlyTestKit.presentCue` now does, out loud. The deeper question — whether a room object should be trainable at all when it can only smell while held — is open.

## ADR 89 — Selecting a thing replaces picking it up, and the smell becomes visible (21.09)

**Status:** accepted. A disclosed override of the same kind as ADR 70, plus the first visual the smell has ever had. `Spectacles-5.23/Assets/Scripts/Fly/FlyAttention.ts`; three call sites in `FlySwarm` (construction, one tick after `presentTick`, one `dbg` term).

- **The open question from ADR 88, answered.** A thing in the room has no smell of its own (`ODOR.object` = sigma 0, strength 0) and only borrows the lure's while `present > 0`. ADR 70 turned `present` on by HOLDING the thing in a palm — impossible for what a room scan actually names: a plant, a curtain, a cabinet, a wall light. So the room inventory was, in practice, untrainable and unsmellable. **Pressing a thing with the SIK cursor (or poking it) is the same act for things you cannot pick up.**
- **The call.** Every sensible source gets a box collider + `Interactable` (`targetingMode = 3`) at its position, sized from `sizeCm` with a 12 cm floor so a small thing is still hittable. A press makes it the ATTENDED one — `present = 1`, held there every frame — a second press releases it and the smell fades over `PRESENT_FADE_S`, exactly as a hand-shown thing does. One attended thing at a time. Nothing else is touched: no steering, no landing gate, no reward.
- **After `presentTick`, on purpose.** Both paths write the same field, so the attended thing's `present = 1` must be the last write of the frame or a hand brushing past would fade what the cursor is holding on.
- **Treats are excluded by label.** They already carry an `InteractableManipulation` (ADR 60) and ADR 70's palm path; a second, bigger collider on top would swallow the grab. Selecting is only for what cannot be picked up.
- **Not hooked at add/remove.** The hit targets re-sync from `WorldSources.items` at 5 Hz, which covers the scanner, the bench, the treats and the ghosts through one path instead of four.
- **The smell was invisible, and it is the whole mechanism.** An attended thing now wears a wireframe box — twelve edges, each drawn twice in two perpendicular planes so no edge vanishes edge-on, 24 quads in 3 `NeonBatch` draws — coloured by its GLOMERULUS: lime VA2 · amber DM4 · magenta DM2 · azure DM1, plus red for `bad` (which has no glomerulus). **Two things that hash to the same glomerulus get the same colour on purpose**: the fly genuinely cannot tell them apart, and hiding that would be the lie.
- **The caption is written for a person, not for the atlas.** `LAPTOP / A SMELL, NOT A SHAPE / only four smells exist in her world / everything amber smells the same / glomerulus DM4`. The glomerulus name stays, last and smallest: true, credible to anyone who knows the atlas, and not the headline. Every line was measured against the real font advances (32–39 cm of a 42 cm budget) and `fitCap` shrinks anything longer — a Gemini label can be any length, and ADR 71's lesson was that a line which does not fit must shrink, never run off.
- **Cost.** 4 extra draws while something is attended or hovered, 0 otherwise; the colliders draw nothing. The quads are rewritten only when the thing, its size or the state changes.

### ADR 89b — ...and what she THINKS of that smell (same day)

Pavlo: "how does a person know whether this smell is good or bad?" The caption said what the smell IS and never what it MEANS to her. One line was added under it. **It takes two signals, because one cannot answer both halves, and pretending otherwise is how a panel starts lying.**

- **Half one — has she learned anything about this smell? Her SYNAPSES, not the rates.** `memory.eff_on` (ADR 67) is the mean efficacy of only the plastic edges whose presynaptic Kenyon cell fired this step — the memory of the smell that is ON, which while a thing is attended is that thing's. It is the drift-free half: synapses no cue drives sit at **1.00083**, a paired cue's own at **0.98705**, an unpaired one at **1.02132**, and the frozen control moves **exactly 0.000**. The gate is `|eff_on − 1| ≥ 0.005` over `n_on ≥ 40` — six times the untouched floor, a third of the smallest real effect. Below it, **"she has no opinion about this yet" is a measurement**, and no amount of output drift can override it.
- **Half two — which way? MBON07 against MBON11, as ELEVATIONS over the rest the brain publishes**, each divided by its own measured non-learning excursion, because 4 cells at 1.5 Hz and 2 cells at 18 Hz are different instruments and one Hz figure would mean something different for each. **MBON07 = 5 Hz** (a live page session sits ~2.5 Hz over its warm-up rest — `mbon07=4.05`; the frozen control moved ±1 Hz; one spike of a 4-cell readout is 1.4 Hz after the brain's 150 ms smoothing). **MBON11 = 25 Hz** (2 cells that DRIFT: 26-40 Hz across a session against a ~18 Hz rest, and **the frozen control swung +12.4 Hz**). The winner must also lead the other by 0.5 of those units; a shown direction holds until its score drops under 0.6; a change must persist 1 s before the caption mesh is rebuilt.
- **Why the thresholds are that high, and what it costs.** ADR 65's own table is the reason. Below ~22 Hz on MBON11 the line would report SESSION DRIFT as "she stays away from this one" — the exact error RUNBOOK warns about ("An MBON11 difference between CS+ and CS- looks like a result") and the same class of error as showing PPL101's 71 Hz rest under the word STRESS. The cost is that **`likes` / `avoids` will rarely fire on today's model**, because in this model learning DEPRESSES the paired compartment (every measured Δ is negative: MBON07 −1.2…−5.2, MBON11 −12.1…−16.5), so an elevation rule cannot see it. That is a fact about the model, not a bug in the line: **the honest UNCLEAR is the right answer until the rate readouts can carry a sign.**
- **So the middle state exists and is the one that will usually show after teaching: `she remembers this smell`** — her synapses for it moved, the outputs do not say which way. Four states in all, plus hidden:

  | state | line | colour |
  |---|---|---|
  | `likes` | `she likes this one` | mint (FlyPalette LIVE) |
  | `avoids` | `she stays away from this one` | orange-red (OFF) |
  | `knows` | `she remembers this smell` | gold (WAIT) |
  | `naive` | `she has no opinion about this yet` | the plain text blue — information, not a verdict |
  | hidden | *(no line)* | the brain reports neither the MBONs-with-a-rest nor `eff_on`; logs `ATTN_NO_VERDICT` once |

  A `bad` thing gets no verdict line: `she turns away from this one` two lines up is INNATE aversion (ORN V/DA2), not something she learned, and stacking a learned verdict on it would blur the two.
- **Found on the way, and it matters: `WebBrainLink` never stores the page's `ready` baseline.** `BrainLink`'s socket path does (`LINK_BASELINE`), but the socket is off (`BRAIN_SOCKET_ENABLED` false) and the page IS the brain (ADR 86) — so `link.baseline` is empty on the glasses, and this session's log has 24 `WEB_BRAIN_ON`, 38 `LINK_SOCKET_OFF` and **zero `LINK_BASELINE`**. The page core does send it (`flybrain.cpp` warm-up emits `"baseline"` for every readout). **The line still works today**, because the `eff_on` half needs no rest — only `likes`/`avoids` are gated on it. One line in `WebBrainLink`'s `p.t === "ready"` branch, mirroring `BrainLink`'s, turns the direction half on.
- **Caveat, stated once:** `eff_on` is the memory of whatever smell is driving Kenyon cells at that step. While a thing is attended its smell is on at lure strength, so it dominates — but a treat lying next to it is in the same average. ADR 65's gotcha still holds too: the four glomeruli share 90-95 % of their Kenyon cells, so "this smell's synapses" is only as cue-specific as the model allows.

## ADR 91 — The fly buzz comes back, on an inverse-distance curve (21.09, Pavlo) — reverses the 12.09 POC "drop the buzzing"

**Status:** accepted. Sound stays brain-driven (loudness = the 67 wing MNs' rate, song only while pIP10 fires); what this ADR decides is the audibility envelope around it, which is ours and disclosed.

- **Why (Pavlo: "звук по дистанції було б круто повернути якщо він прибраний").** It was never removed by design, and it was not audible either — three stacked causes, found from code, the scene and the logs:
  1. `SOUND_ENABLED: false` since the 12.09 POC pass ("drop the buzzing too, it is surplus right now"): FlySwarm never built `FlySound`.
  2. `fly_buzz.wav` / `fly_song.wav` (+ metas) went missing from the project the same evening (inside an unrelated commit). `Scene.scene` still referenced their ids (`8b6c7585…`, `c80a05f5…`), so `buzzTrack` / `songTrack` resolve to nothing and FlySwarm's `(buzzTrack || songTrack)` gate stays false even with the flag on — silence, no error anywhere.
  3. Even when it played (12.09 telemetry: `buzz=0.12 wingMax=38 wing=30.3`) the envelope was tiny: master 0.28 (halved by Pavlo the same day the buzz was declared surplus) × a `((FAR−d)/(FAR−NEAR))²` ramp on 60/400 cm that is 0.78 at 1 m, 0.35 at 2 m, **0.09 at 3 m, 0 at 4 m**. A giant fly across the room was inaudible on the glasses' speakers.
- **The call.** `SOUND_ENABLED` on; master `SOUND_BUZZ_VOL` 0.5; distance = full within `SOUND_NEAR_CM` 100, inverse distance `(NEAR/d)^SOUND_ROLLOFF` (1) beyond, a smoothstep fade over the last `SOUND_FADE_CM` 100 before `SOUND_FAR_CM` 500 — **1 m 1.00 · 2 m 0.50 · 3 m 0.33 · 4 m 0.25 · 5 m 0** ("a fat fly a metre away"). Wing gain floor `SOUND_WING_MIN_GAIN` 0.35 (a flying fly always buzzes), escape `SOUND_ESCAPE_GAIN` 1.4, auto-range floor `SOUND_WING_FLOOR_HZ` 30 — the wing MNs run **28–48 Hz in flight (median 39, 318 telemetry rows)**, so the old 5 Hz floor made the first reading "the peak" — forgetting with `SOUND_WING_PEAK_TAU_S` 20 s. Spatial audio stays off (12.09 CPU call), and the engine's `DistanceEffect` stays off on purpose: its default `maxDistance` is 100 cm; our curve is the one that counts.
- **What is engineered here, plainly:** the distance envelope, the master, the speed factor and the escape gain. The brain still decides WHEN it buzzes (flying vs landed, escape) and HOW hard (wing MN rate); nothing here moves the fly or invents a wingbeat.
- **Visible in the log:** `SOUND_READY` once (tracks yes/NO, spatial, the curve sampled at 1–4 m), `SOUND_START loops=N` after the intro, `SOUND_NO_TRACK` when nothing is wired, and a `SOUND` row every `SOUND_LOG_S` 5 s while `DEBUG_TELEMETRY_S > 0` (per fly: state, `wing=hz/peak`, speed, distance, gain, buzz, song). The dbg row's `sound off` means FlySwarm never built it.
- **Done since:** both wav files are back in `Assets/Fly/Audio/` of both projects with their ORIGINAL metas and wired as `buzzTrack` / `songTrack`. **Open:** the loudness is judged by ear on the glasses, not in the preview.

## ADR 92 — The memory trace fades over 3 hours, not 30 minutes (21.09, Pavlo: "так ок 3 год давай")

- **What.** The plastic KC→MBON trace (`mem_u`, rule.py's `memory_decay_seconds`) relaxes with τ = **10800 s** instead of 1800 s. The value is `rule[2]` in the brain file, so it was changed in the FILE (`brain_c0.flyb.z` and its twin `brain_full.flyb.z`, patched in place: rule `[1, 1, 1800, 0.05, 0.1, 2, 0.001]` → `[1, 1, 10800, …]`, backups `*.tau1800.bak`) and in the exporter (`export.py` `MEMORY_DECAY_S = 10800`), never in the lens or the page. Nothing else in the rule moved.
- **Why.** Pavlo saw "MEMORY — fades in ~30 min" on the board and asked why it fades at all. Forgetting is the rule's own hygiene: now that she learns by herself all the time (ADR 89), without decay every accidental pairing would pile up forever (4,119 of 7,835 synapses moved in ten minutes with no session). But half an hour threw away a room she was still standing in. Three hours is the range of a real fly's single-trial memory; what she keeps smelling is refreshed and stays, what is gone fades.
- **Consequences.** A stored memory's catch-up on load (ADR 63's closed form) uses the file's τ, so old blobs simply decay slower from now on. The page must re-download the brain to pick the new file up (a cached copy keeps 1800 s until then). Legend copy: "fades by itself in about 3 hours". Later, if wanted: a second, slow trace for repeated pairings (days), like a real long-term memory.

## ADR 93 — Everything the scan finds smells, and a plain thing's smell is identity-only (21.09, Pavlo: "все має пахнути в тій чи іншій мірі все що геміні знаходить") — extends #16, #26, #65; supersedes the "a thing in the room has no smell of its own" finding of #88 / #89

**Status:** accepted. An engineered input, disclosed: it gives the brain a signal, it decides nothing.

- **Before.** `ODOR.object` was `{sigma 0, strength 0}`: a laptop, a vase, a cabinet smelled only while HELD (#70) or ATTENDED (#89). The scanned room was unsmellable, so nothing in it could be learned about unless a person pointed at it — "по суті ми ніфіга не вчимо муху".
- **Now.** Gemini returns a `smell` 0..1 for every object (prompt + schema in `WorldScanner.ts`: food / plants / kitchen / bins 1; wood, paper, fabric, leather 0.6; plastic, painted furniture 0.3; glass, metal, screens 0; `OBJECT_SMELL_DEFAULT` 0.5 when it gives none). `ODOR.object` = `{sigma 70, strength 0.6}` scaled: strength x smell, sigma x (0.5 + 0.5 smell) -> 35-70 cm (`WorldSources.setProps`). A wooden table at 30 cm ≈ 0.4, over the ~0.36 KC firing floor (#65's sweep); a plastic chair peaks at 0.18 and never fires — "faint" is faint. Big furniture is allowed in the scan now (walls / floor / ceiling still skipped: no box position).
- **The call that keeps #26 intact.** A plain `object`'s smell goes ONLY into its identity glomerulus (`idL/idR` in `FlySenses.compute`) — never into the generic appetitive `odor` sum, never hunger-scaled, never into `odorLevel` (so `forage` cannot chase a sofa). What the mushroom body gets is one glomerulus's cue that a reward or a bitter contact on landing can attach to — the self-directed learning Pavlo means. A presented / attended thing still borrows the lure's smell exactly as before.
- **Cost.** Objects used to be skipped by `str > 0`; now they pay the same two exponentials every other smell pays. Nothing allocated.
- **Not verified.** Whether one glomerulus at ≤ 0.6 moves any behaviour is a page-brain measurement to make (one glomerulus at 0.9 fires ~1,500 KCs, #65), and nothing here was seen on the glasses.

## ADR 102 — The wearer smells, and a scare near the wearer is a lesson (21.09, Pavlo: "якщо я загроза то муха вчиться простіше мене ідентифікувати") — extends #33, #65, #93; the first autonomous aversive pairing

**Status:** accepted. Two disclosed engineered inputs: a smell on the wearer, and a `punish` US on a real threat event. The brain still decides what to do with both.

- **Why.** Escape from a looming hand is INNATE (LC4/LPLC2 → DNp01, #33) and never sharpens: there was nothing for the mushroom body to attach a scare to, because the wearer had no smell (`ODOR.threat` sigma 0) and no US was ever sent outside a session. Memory in this model is smell-only (#65), so "learning to identify the threat" can only mean: her smell of the human comes to mean danger.
- **The smell.** `FlySwarm.humanize` gives `user_head` and both hands ONE odour identity — `labelOdour("human")`, so the three body parts are one smell — at `HUMAN_SMELL` 0.5, sigma `HUMAN_SMELL_HEAD_CM` 60 / `HUMAN_SMELL_HAND_CM` 25. `FlySenses.compute` routes class `threat` exactly like a plain `object` (#93): identity glomerulus ONLY, no appetitive `odor`, no hunger gain, no `odorLevel`, so she cannot forage toward a person. Log `THREAT_LESSON fly= v= s= why=loom left_hand loom=0.71 energy= human=0.23` once per pulse. **The detectors are the existing loom and touch signals** — nothing new senses anything.

## ADR 103 — The reward follows her hunger, real food pays it, and discomfort is aversive (21.09, Pavlo: "хай вона сама змінює той гриб постійно, в залежності від кондицій, як це в реальному світі") — extends #26, #42, #88, #95; scales a US the lens already fabricated

- **Reward by hunger.** In a fly, hunger gates appetitive learning and its expression (a fed fly barely learns that a smell means sugar, and does not act on it). `FlySwarm.rewardGain(energy)` = `REWARD_FED` 0.2 at 100 % energy, 1.0 at or below `REWARD_FULL_BELOW` 0.4, linear between. - **Discomfort.** With `hot` or `cold` at the fly ≥ `DISCOMFORT_AT` 0.6 (after #95's baseline and #96's local fields), a `punish` of `DISCOMFORT_PUNISH` 0.5 for `DISCOMFORT_S` 0.3 s every `DISCOMFORT_EVERY_S` 6 s — heat-shock conditioning, paired with whatever she smells there. 0.5 because below ~0.35 a sense channel is silent (#31). - **Not verified:** on a brain. Expected: `memory.rewards` climbs on food landings; a fed fly's landing prints `rewardGain=0.2x` and moves `eff_on` a fifth as far as a starved one's.

## ADR 107 — A dropped brain is shown, not hidden: the hologram blinks out, the header says DROPPED and keeps the PIN (21.09, Pavlo: "цікаво що в лінзах стає коли відключається мозок… треба візуалізувати що мозок відпав") — extends #55, #87; refines "no brain, no fly"

- **What.** When the brain link goes from up to down while the fly is shown, `FlySwarm` does not withhold her between two frames: `tickBlink` blinks the fly's root on/off for `BRAIN_LOST_BLINK_S` (1.8 s; bursts whose period and duty shrink toward the end), then applies the plain rule (withheld). A link that comes back blinks her in over `BRAIN_BACK_BLINK_S` (0.6 s). Log markers `BRAIN_LOST` / `BRAIN_LOST_HIDDEN` / `BRAIN_BACK` / `BRAIN_BACK_SHOWN`. The board header reads `BRAIN: DROPPED · reopen the page` (a link that was never up still reads NOT CONNECTED) and the PIN chip turns into the call to action `OPEN THE WEB PAGE · PIN nnnn`; the last brain message no longer counts as "on your web page" once the link is down.
- **Why.** A fly that vanishes silently reads as a crash. The blink is the one hologram idiom everyone knows for "signal lost", it costs nothing (visibility only, no material value: ADR 17 holds), and the header tells the person what to do.
- **Not a behaviour override.** No motion is invented: for 1.8 s the body keeps its last decoded commands (the core's stale-sense rule, ADR 104, still applies), and after that there is no fly, as before.
- **Test.** LEAF `brain_lost` (5.23) drives `FlySwarm.debugLinkStatus(false/true)`: both states seen inside the blink, hidden after it, shown again after the return.
